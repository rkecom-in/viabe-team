# Sales Methods Taxonomy for Viabe Sales Agent

Created: 2026-07-26

Purpose: a retrieval-ready map of sales methods currently used across businesses. This is not a script to follow blindly. It is a tactical library the Sales Agent can use to choose, combine, adapt, and invent sales plays for Indian SMBs.

Core principle: choose the sales method from the business situation, not from fashion. The relevant variables are buyer type, ticket size, margin, urgency, trust gap, sales cycle, fulfilment capacity, payment terms, and available proof.

## 1. Named sales methodologies

These are structured ways to run conversations and qualify deals.

| Method | What it teaches | Best fit | Avoid / modify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| BANT | Qualify Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline quickly. | High-volume inbound, simple B2B, first qualification calls. | Bad fit for complex buying committees where budget/authority are fluid. |
| CHAMP | Start with Challenges before Authority, Money, Prioritization. | Buyer-problem-led SMB consultative sales. | Too light for enterprise procurement. |
| SPIN Selling | Ask Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff questions. | Consultative sales where buyer does not fully feel the cost of the problem. | Buyer already knows need and wants price/availability. |
| Challenger | Teach, tailor, and take control by reframing the buyer’s thinking. | B2B where status quo is the competitor and seller has strong insight. | Low-trust or highly relationship-driven contexts unless softened. |
| Sandler | Up-front contract, pain discovery, budget, decision process, mutual qualification. | Complex SMB/B2B where reps waste time on bad-fit prospects. | Very transactional/low-ticket sales where structure slows conversion. |
| MEDDIC | Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion. | Mid-market / enterprise B2B pipeline qualification. | Too heavy for small-ticket, owner-led, one-call sales. |
| MEDDPICC | MEDDIC plus Paper Process and Competition. | Enterprise, procurement, high-ACV, multi-stakeholder deals. | SMB deals where paperwork complexity is low. |
| Consultative Selling | Diagnose first, prescribe second. | Services, agencies, finance, compliance, high-trust categories. | Buyer wants a commodity and has already decided. |
| Solution Selling | Bundle product/service around a diagnosed business problem. | B2B or service sales with multiple components. | Risks becoming vague unless problem and ROI are quantified. |
| Value-Based Selling | Sell economic/business value, not features. | Higher-ticket B2B, owner ROI decisions, expensive services. | Hard when value cannot be measured or proof is weak. |
| Gap Selling | Show current state, future state, and cost of the gap. | Change-management sales and operational improvement offers. | Weak if seller cannot prove gap cost. |
| SNAP Selling | Keep it Simple, iNvaluable, Aligned, Priority-focused. | Busy buyers, short attention, overwhelmed owners. | Deep enterprise discovery needs heavier structure. |
| SPICED | Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision. | Customer-success-led expansion and lifecycle selling. | Early unknown-market sales may need more research first. |
| NEAT | Need, Economic impact, Access to authority, Timeline. | Qualification where ROI and authority matter. | Transactional sales where economic impact is small. |
| GPCT / GPCTBA-CI | Goals, Plans, Challenges, Timeline, Budget, Authority, Consequences, Implications. | Inbound/consultative sales and owner goal discovery. | Too verbose for quick retail/local selling. |
| Strategic / Miller-Heiman style selling | Map stakeholders, influence, competition, and deal strategy. | Large accounts, government/corporate procurement, multi-role committees. | Too much overhead for small deals. |
| Account-Based Selling | Treat each target account as a market of one. | High-value B2B, corporate gifting, agencies, enterprise suppliers. | Broad low-ticket consumer markets. |
| Social Selling | Use networks, authority, and helpful engagement to open conversations. | B2B, creators, professional services, community-heavy niches. | Spammy promotion or weak proof. |

## 2. Route-to-market motions

These are ways businesses actually acquire customers.

| Motion | How it works | Strong when | Weak when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inbound / content-led | Helpful content, SEO, videos, guides, calculators, FAQs attract buyers. | Buyer searches before buying; trust gap is high. | Urgent demand needed today; content runway too long. |
| Outbound prospecting | Cold/warm email, LinkedIn, calls, WhatsApp where lawful/consented, direct visits. | Named buyer list exists; B2B or local decision makers identifiable. | No clear ICP or weak reason to respond. |
| Referral selling | Ask happy customers/partners for introductions. | Trust matters and buyer networks cluster. | Product experience is weak or customer satisfaction unproven. |
| Word of mouth | Make customer experience remarkable enough to spread. | Local, services, food, beauty, trust categories. | No review/referral capture process. |
| Local discovery | Google Business Profile, Maps, local SEO, listings, photos, reviews. | Location/service-area businesses. | Business sells nationally or category is not local-search driven. |
| Walk-in / retail conversion | Storefront, signage, merchandising, staff scripts, demos, sampling. | Physical retail, restaurants, salons, clinics. | No footfall or poor location economics. |
| Field sales / door-to-door | Reps visit shops, offices, homes, societies, markets. | Hyperlocal, trust-heavy, product needs demonstration. | Ticket too low for visit cost or safety/compliance risk. |
| Inside sales / telesales | Phone/video-led selling with CRM cadence. | Repeatable offer, clear lists, moderate ticket size. | Buyer requires physical proof or relationship first. |
| Conversational commerce | WhatsApp/DM-based discovery, reminders, quotes, catalog, support. | India SMBs, repeat customers, service follow-ups. | No consent, no reply capacity, poor template quality. |
| Social commerce | Instagram/Facebook/YouTube/WhatsApp discovery and purchase. | Visual products, D2C, creators, local retail. | Product is complex or needs procurement approval. |
| Marketplace selling | Amazon/Flipkart/ONDC/IndiaMART/Udaan/Meesho-type discovery. | Buyer already searches marketplace; trust/logistics provided by platform. | Margins cannot absorb fees/returns/ads. |
| Trade fairs / exhibitions | Concentrated offline buyer discovery and lead capture. | B2B, wholesale, craft, manufacturing, export, corporate gifting. | No pre-event targeting or follow-up discipline. |
| Buyer-seller meets | Structured B2B meetings through chambers, MSME, associations. | Suppliers need distributor/corporate/government buyers. | Product/catalog/pricing not ready. |
| Channel sales | Dealers, distributors, retailers, agents resell. | Geography expansion and recurring physical distribution. | Brand/control/margin leakage not managed. |
| Partnerships / co-selling | Sell with complementary businesses. | Shared audience, high trust, natural bundle. | Partner incentive unclear or customer ownership conflict. |
| Affiliate / influencer / creator selling | Third party promotes for fee/commission/relationship. | D2C, niche audiences, trust-based categories. | Claims compliance, attribution, or unit economics weak. |
| Product-led growth | Free tool/trial/freemium lets users experience value before sales. | Software/digital products with self-serve value. | Product cannot show value without heavy service. |
| Demo / trial / sample-led selling | Prospect experiences product before buying. | Sensory, high-trust, new product, quality differentiation. | Samples are costly and qualification is weak. |
| Pilot / POC selling | Limited proof-of-concept before larger contract. | B2B/high-ticket solutions where buyer needs proof. | Scope creep or no success criteria. |
| Procurement / RFP / tender selling | Formal bid process with eligibility, pricing, documents. | Government, corporate, institutional buyers. | Documentation weak or margins cannot survive L1 pricing pressure. |
| Key account management | Deepen and protect large existing accounts. | Few customers drive large revenue. | Business ignores smaller but higher-margin segments. |
| Customer success expansion | Upsell/cross-sell after outcome delivery. | Subscription/services/B2B repeat revenue. | Initial value not delivered. |
| Winback / reactivation | Recover lapsed customers. | Repeat-purchase categories with dormant base. | Customer left due to unresolved complaint or bad experience. |
| Retention / loyalty selling | Memberships, points, subscriptions, prepaid packs. | Repeat-use categories. | Discount/points destroy margin or add accounting friction. |
| Community-led selling | Build audience/community around problem/identity. | Passion/niche categories, education, creator-led brands. | Community is only used as promotion channel. |
| Education/workshop selling | Teach first, sell after trust. | Complex services, B2B, health/fitness/finance/learning. | Content does not naturally lead to offer. |

## 3. Tactical levers the Sales Agent can compose

These are smaller tools that can be combined into new plays.

| Lever | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ICP narrowing | Reduce waste by selecting the right buyer slice. | “Boutique hotels in Jaipur with wedding season demand,” not “hotels.” |
| Trigger-event selling | Contact when buyer need is newly active. | New store opening, festival season, hiring surge, compliance notice, funding, relocation. |
| Occasion-based selling | Tie offer to calendar/use-case. | Diwali gifting, wedding season, school reopening, monsoon maintenance. |
| Pain-cost quantification | Make inaction expensive. | Lost walk-ins from wrong hours, stockouts, late payment interest, ad waste. |
| Risk reversal | Reduce fear of purchase. | Trial pack, sample, warranty, COD, first-month review, pilot milestone. |
| Social proof | Borrow trust from others. | Reviews, case studies, before/after, corporate logos, testimonials. |
| Scarcity / urgency | Legitimate reason to act now. | Limited seats, cut-off date, seasonal inventory. Never fake it. |
| Bundling | Increase perceived value/AOV. | Starter kit, festive bundle, service package. |
| Unbundling | Lower entry barrier. | Paid audit before full project, sample box before bulk order. |
| Tiered pricing | Let buyer self-select. | Good/better/best, basic/pro/premium. |
| Anchoring | Frame value comparison. | Compare annual maintenance cost vs breakdown cost. |
| Land-and-expand | Start small, grow inside account. | Pilot one branch, then all branches. |
| Multi-threading | Build relationships with more than one stakeholder. | User, buyer, finance, owner, procurement. |
| Champion building | Equip internal supporter to sell internally. | ROI note, comparison sheet, WhatsApp summary, demo video. |
| Objection library | Systematically handle repeated doubts. | Price, trust, delivery, warranty, switching effort, GST invoice. |
| Follow-up cadence | Prevent silent drop after first contact. | Day 0, 2, 5, 10 with different value each time. |
| Lead scoring | Prioritize likely buyers. | Fit, urgency, budget, authority, engagement, repeat potential. |
| Offer testing | Test hook/price/channel before scaling. | Two audiences, two offers, one variable changed. |
| Review flywheel | Convert happy customers into proof. | Ask, link, respond, reuse insight ethically. |
| Reactivation hook | Give dormant buyer a reason to return. | New arrival, replenishment reminder, complaint recovery, seasonal need. |
| Cross-sell map | Pair products/services by natural next need. | Shoes + polish, gym + diet consult, cafe + cake preorder. |
| Offline-to-online capture | Turn footfall into future audience. | QR catalog, WhatsApp opt-in, review QR, event lead form. |
| Online-to-offline conversion | Move digital interest into visit/call/demo. | Appointment booking, store pickup, free consultation. |

## 4. Method selection rules

Use these as Sales Agent decision heuristics.

### Low-ticket consumer / local

Prefer: local discovery, reviews, WhatsApp, walk-in conversion, referral, bundles, loyalty, occasion campaigns.

Avoid: heavy enterprise qualification frameworks.

### High-ticket services

Prefer: consultative selling, SPIN, value-based selling, audit/diagnosis, proof, referrals, case studies.

Avoid: generic discount blasts.

### B2B SMB

Prefer: outbound named lists, CHAMP/BANT, SPIN discovery, referral, sample/demo, WhatsApp/email follow-up, payment-term clarity.

Avoid: pitching “companies” without mapping decision-maker role.

### Corporate / enterprise

Prefer: account-based selling, MEDDIC/MEDDPICC, Challenger insight, multi-threading, champion enablement, procurement mapping, POC.

Avoid: single-thread relationship dependence.

### Marketplace / ecommerce

Prefer: marketplace search optimization, pricing/packaging, reviews, ads with contribution-margin checks, post-purchase upsell, returns discipline.

Avoid: scaling ads before unit economics and return rate are known.

### New product / low awareness

Prefer: education-led content, demos, samples, community listening, problem reframing, pilots.

Avoid: assuming buyers understand the category.

### Cashflow-constrained SMB

Prefer: direct outreach, referrals, local partnerships, WhatsApp to consented base, reactivation, low-cost events.

Avoid: broad paid campaigns without tracking.

## 5. Innovation primitives

To invent a new sales tactic, combine one item from each row.

| Primitive | Choices |
|---|---|
| Buyer slice | local residents, lapsed customers, nearby offices, HR/admin buyers, wedding planners, distributors, high-LTV existing customers |
| Trigger | festival, expiry, stockout, new branch, complaint resolved, seasonal pain, compliance deadline, payment cycle |
| Trust asset | review, testimonial, demo, sample, certification, GST invoice, before/after, case study |
| Offer | audit, starter pack, bundle, trial, pilot, sample, consultation, subscription, annual plan |
| Channel | WhatsApp, phone, LinkedIn, email, walk-in, local event, trade fair, marketplace, partner, community |
| Risk control | opt-in, budget cap, margin check, fulfilment capacity, legal/compliance check, owner approval |
| Follow-up | reminder, proof, objection answer, deadline, alternate offer, referral ask |

Example innovation:

For a local bakery before Diwali: “nearby offices” + “festival gifting” + “sample box” + “corporate bundle” + “WhatsApp/visit” + “margin check” + “48-hour follow-up.” This becomes an office-gifting pipeline play, not a generic discount campaign.
