# VT-773 — a link one customer can click, and a link a whole campaign can click, are different objects

## The finding (Codex's, in its Ad Composer design — and it is right)

`integrations/hook_links.py` mints `/r/<token>` where the token is **bound to a customer** and the
redirect target is **a tenant's WhatsApp number**. Both properties are wrong for an ad or a
campaign-wide link:

- **Customer-bound** — an ad impression has no customer yet. Minting one token per unknown clicker is
  incoherent, and reusing a single customer's token across an audience would attribute a whole
  campaign to one person and leak that person's identity into a public destination.
- **WhatsApp-destined** — an ad click should reach a Reports landing/checkout page, not a chat.

Codex's Ad Composer therefore emits a typed `TrackedDestinationRequest` instead of fabricating a URL,
and fails closed when no mint exists. **That is the correct behaviour and this row is what unblocks
it** — not a change on its side.

## What this seam is

A **campaign-scoped, non-customer-bound** tracked destination:

- mints against a *campaign/artifact*, never a customer — no PII in or behind the token;
- resolves to an **allowlisted** destination route (Reports landing / checkout), never a chat and
  never a caller-supplied arbitrary URL;
- carries canonical UTM components (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`),
  normalised, with no PII permitted in any component;
- records clicks into the SAME attribution ledger as the customer-scoped links, so P1.4's
  err-under attribution has one home rather than two.

**The two link types must stay structurally distinct**, not one function with a nullable customer_id.
They differ in privacy class: one identifies a person, the other must not be able to. A nullable
column is how that distinction gets lost in six months.

## Who is blocked on it
**P2.5 Ad Composer** (its destination request has nothing to resolve against) · **P2.4 Content**
(landing/social links want the same aggregate object) · **P1.5's campaign-offer template**, whose
URL button needs an aggregate destination when the send is a campaign rather than a 1:1 win-back.

## Exit gate
(a) A campaign destination can be minted and resolved with **no customer binding anywhere in the
token or its lookup** — proven by a test asserting the mint refuses a customer_id argument entirely
rather than accepting and ignoring it. (b) Destination routes are **allowlisted**; a caller-supplied
full URL or arbitrary query string is rejected. (c) UTM components are normalised and **PII-rejecting**
— proven with a phone number and an email as inputs, both refused. (d) Clicks land in the same
attribution ledger as `/r/<token>` clicks and are distinguishable by link class. (e) `/r/<token>` is
**unchanged** — this row adds an object, it does not generalise the customer-scoped one.

## Deliberately NOT in scope
Changing `hook_links.py`'s customer-scoped behaviour. VT-745's click infra is correct for what it
does; the mistake would be to widen it until it serves both and is safe for neither.
