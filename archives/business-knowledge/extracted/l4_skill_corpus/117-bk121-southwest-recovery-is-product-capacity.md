## Situation

Winter weather triggered Southwest's 2022 disruption, but recovery, notification, refund and reimbursement failed at scale. US DOT action covered more than two million passengers and a $140 million settlement.

## Decision pressure

Normal-day utilisation rewards lean recovery staffing and fragile fallbacks because contingency capacity looks idle.

## Mistake or risk

Do not label every disruption a technology failure or let the external trigger excuse preventable recovery harm. Separate prevention, containment, recovery and remedy.

## Recommended next action

Model peak disruption; test degraded scheduling and manual authority; reserve recovery/communication capacity; predefine refund/remedy workflows; exercise through full backlog clearance.

## Evidence needed

- Dependency and disruption scenarios
- Recovery throughput versus backlog
- Fallback data, staffing and authority
- Notification and remedy timing

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Continuity test stops at restoration
- Fallback cannot reconcile operations
- Support is excluded from resilience
- No remedy authority during disruption

## Agent lesson

Operations and Sales protect recovery and remedy, Finance funds severe-event capacity, and the Manager treats recovery as part of the promise.

## Hard-gate candidate

Critical operations require tested degraded mode, backlog-clearance capacity, communication and remedy authority.
