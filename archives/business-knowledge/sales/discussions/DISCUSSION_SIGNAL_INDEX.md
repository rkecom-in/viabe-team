## Conditional tactic matrix

| Condition | Better-fit tactics | Weak / risky tactics | Agent lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| First 10 B2B customers, no audience | Founder-led discovery, Dream-10 list, free diagnostic, warm referrals, narrow ICP | Generic LinkedIn/email blasts, outsourced sales before founder learning | The first sale is also market research; outsource too early and the agent loses the truth. |
| High-trust or new-category sale | Custom demo, pilot, workshop, founder call, proof using prospect data | Pure self-serve funnel, low-context cold pitch | Reduce buyer risk before asking for a large commitment. |
| Low-ticket local business | Google Business Profile, reviews, UGC, local partnerships, WhatsApp repeat-order loop, sampling | Broad paid search/social ads without repeat economics | Local memory and repeat buying beat paid reach when margin is thin. |
| Niche product with gathered community | Native educational posts, tutorials, community contribution, creator partnerships | Drive-by self-promotion, link drops, obvious astroturfing | Community distribution works only when the business has earned attention. |
| Ecommerce/DTC with rising ad costs | Unit-economics audit, offer testing, retention, email/SMS/WhatsApp lifecycle, micro-influencers, community | Scaling ads before contribution margin and conversion are proven | Digital marketing amplifies the business model; it does not rescue a weak one. |
| Low ACV SaaS / subscription | SEO, content, product-led loop, referrals, onboarding, template/gallery virality | Heavy outbound after early validation | Human sales time must match annual value. |
| Partner-accessible B2B | Integrators, complementary partners, channel co-selling, vertical resellers | Building only a direct sales motion when buyers already trust someone else | Borrow trust and distribution where the buyer already has a relationship. |
| Physical/offline-dense market | Events, demos, sampling, trade fairs, local media, signage, QR/WhatsApp capture | Offline activity without capture/follow-up | Physical marketing creates trust; digital follow-up compounds it. |

## Campaign success heuristics

Successful campaigns tend to have all of these:

- A specific buyer and situation, not a generic audience.
- A painful or desirable trigger that makes action timely.
- A clear offer with low-friction next step.
- Proof that matches the trust gap: reviews, case studies, demo, sample, guarantee, certification, founder credibility, or community reputation.
- Channel fit: the buyer already spends attention there, and the format feels native to that place.
- Follow-up loop: captured contact, remarketing audience, WhatsApp/email, review request, referral ask, or repeat purchase path.
- Measurement discipline: CAC, conversion rate, payback period, repeat rate, gross margin, campaign-specific code/UTM/QR/phone tracking.
- Brand memory: consistent promise, name/visual assets, distinctive category language, and repeated useful presence.

## Digital vs physical marketing nuance

Digital marketing is strong at targeting, measurement, fast iteration, retargeting, content compounding, and narrow audience reach. Its weakness is trust: many buyers ignore polished ads because they look like every other ad, and platform CAC can rise quickly.

Physical marketing is strong at trust, locality, sampling, memorability, relationship-building, and “I saw them in my area” credibility. Its weakness is measurement and scale unless every offline touchpoint captures a digital follow-up path.

For SMB agents, the best answer is often hybrid:

- Use physical activity to create proof and trust: demo, sampling, event, local partner, customer photo, in-store poster.
- Use digital systems to capture and compound: QR to WhatsApp, review request, coupon code, landing page, CRM tag, retargeting audience, referral prompt.
- Measure by cohort or source code, not vibes.
