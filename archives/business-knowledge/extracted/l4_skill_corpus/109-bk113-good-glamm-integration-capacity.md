## Situation

Good Glamm assembled eleven acquisitions; its founder later described moving too much, too fast and too big, while reporting documented restructuring, delayed payments and distressed asset sales.

## Decision pressure

Capital, deal momentum and a platform narrative reward closing the next acquisition before earlier businesses share systems, cash discipline or a coherent proposition.

## Mistake or risk

Acquisitions are not inherently unsound and later distress rarely has one cause. The usable mechanism is deal pace exceeding integration capacity and depending on future funding.

## Recommended next action

Quantify standalone and integration cash, name an owner, define 30/90/180-day milestones, cap concurrent integrations, protect payroll and vendors, and stop new deals when prior milestones miss.

## Evidence needed

- Standalone and consolidated runway
- Measured synergy thesis
- Integration owner and capacity
- Working-capital and debt obligations
- Brand overlap and channel conflict

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Deal depends on the next fundraise
- No integration owner
- Payroll or vendors fund expansion
- Synergy has no baseline or deadline

## Agent lesson

Finance owns cash depth, Marketing owns portfolio coherence, and the Manager gates deal pace to integration capacity.

## Hard-gate candidate

No new acquisition while existing integrations miss material cash, customer, data or operating milestones without a recovery plan.

## Retrieval triggers

- too many acquisitions

- house of brands failure
- Good Glamm
- acquisition cashflow
