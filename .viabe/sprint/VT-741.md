# VT-741 — how often we may message one customer

## THE RULE (ratified — build exactly this)

**Evaluated in order. FIRST MATCH WINS. Stop at the first tier that matches.**
The ordering is not stylistic: **Tier A is a strict subset of Tier B by construction** (reply/click
⊂ read-or-click-or-reply). Evaluating B first would swallow every A customer and silently cap the
most engaged cohort at 3 days. Fazal called this out explicitly and required the correction.

| Order | Tier | Interval | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **A — engaged** | **once / 24h** | REPLIED or CLICKED a tracked link in any of the **last 3** messages sent to this customer, **and that reply/click occurred within the last 30 days** |
| 2nd | **B — aware** | **once / 3 days** | READ or CLICKED or REPLIED to any of the **last 10** messages sent to this customer, **and that signal occurred within the last 90 days** |
| 3rd | **C — cold** | **once / 7 days** | everyone else — the default |

**Time bounds (Fazal-approved 2026-08-10):** 30 days on A, 90 days on B. Without them the rule has
no floor: a customer who replied once eight months ago sits in Tier A forever if we have sent them
only three messages since. For a low-volume tenant that is a spam generator compiled into the rule.

## Binding semantics — the parts that are easy to get wrong

1. **This is a SUPPRESSION layer, never an authorization.** It can only ever make us send LESS. It
   never causes, permits or unblocks a send. Consent, opt-out, onboarded, ownership_verified and
   Pillar-7 approval all sit UPSTREAM and are untouched. A customer in Tier A with no consent gets
   nothing — the tier is irrelevant to them.
2. **Fail-closed, always to Tier C.** Unreadable engagement data, an error, a partial read, a
   missing table — all resolve to **once / 7 days**, never to the permissive tier. There is no
   error path that increases send rate.
3. **A brand-new customer (zero messages sent) is Tier C.** Most conservative by default. Engagement
   must be earned, never assumed.
4. **"Last N messages"** = the last N OUTBOUND messages from this tenant to this customer, ordered
   by sent-at. Per (tenant, customer) — never global, never cross-tenant.
5. **Absence of a read receipt is NOT evidence of not reading.** WhatsApp read receipts are
   user-disableable. Such customers can never satisfy B's `read` clause, so for them Tier B
   effectively does not exist — they are Tier A (replied/clicked) or Tier C. **Accepted, known,
   documented.** Do not "fix" it by inferring engagement from delivery.
6. **A blocked customer is a HARD STOP, not a tier.** When sends to a customer begin failing in the
   block/spam class, that customer is permanently suppressed and the failure marks the tenant's
   quality score. Do not let a blocked customer sit in any tier retrying weekly.
7. **Enforced at the SEND CHOKE**, per (tenant, customer) — not per campaign, not per path. Three
   automatic re-drive paths exist (reaper wake · `approval_resume.redrive_task` · operator redrive,
   VT-740); a suppression that lives on one path is not a suppression.
8. **The interval is a CONFIGURED VALUE read from policy, not a literal in the code.** Same socket
   whether the number later comes from the Manager, an owner grant or config. This is the whole
   reason the deterministic-now / intelligent-later switch costs nothing.

## THE SIGNALS — the actual build. We have one of three.

**REPLY — probably have it, VERIFY DO NOT ASSUME.** `integrations.customer_inbound.handle_customer_inbound`
exists and VT-287 is Done. But "inbound is handled" and "a reply is durably attributed to (tenant,
customer, message) as an engagement signal" are different claims, and the standing scar is that
inbound was owner-only for a long time (`subscriber_states` PK `tenant_id`). **Verify against the
schema before building on it.**

**READ — captured on the wire, then THROWN AWAY.** `agents/customer_send.py:970`:
```python
_DELIVERY_STATE_MAP = {"delivered": "delivered", "read": "delivered", ...}
```
Twilio delivers a distinct `read` callback and we collapse it into `delivered` at the map. The
signal the entire middle tier depends on is arriving today and being discarded one line before it
would be persisted. `mig 161` CHECK constrains `delivery_status`, so preserving `read` needs a
migration — allocate the number, never hand-pick (CL-424).

**CLICK — does not exist for customer messages. This is the real build.**
No link tracking on any customer-facing message. **But the pattern exists and is proven:**
`integrations/hook_links.py` (VT-288, migration 071) — a tokenised link we own at `/r/<token>`,
resolved token→tenant SERVER-SIDE, click recorded, 302 to the destination. **Extend that; do not
invent a second one.**

**Two constraints on the click extension that are not negotiable:**
- **The token IS the capability. No customer id, tenant id, campaign id or phone in the URL.** The
  redirect resolves token → (tenant, customer, message) server-side. `hook_links` is deny-all RLS,
  service-role-only, explicitly no-PII (CL-390) — a customer-attributed click table is a DIFFERENT
  privacy class and needs its own RLS + FORCE, not that one's posture.
- **Click records are customer PII. Register them in `_PURGE_ORDER` IN THE SAME MIGRATION.** DSR
  anonymizes the tenants row rather than deleting it, so no FK cascade will ever clean this up. This
  is the recurring scar (episodic_events, L2) and it is forgotten every single time.

## Why DETERMINISTIC now, and what it deliberately gives up

Fazal's 05:30 direction was that the Manager should own product/policy calls — *"the intelligence
has to wind through the entire Tenants business."* He then chose the hardcoded rule **for now**.
That is not a retreat, and the row records why so nobody re-opens it as a compromise:

**What the deterministic rule buys:** no reasoning path into cadence, therefore **no prompt-injection
route to a send-rate increase** (a hostile inbound claiming "the owner approved daily contact" is
inert against a rule); no self-widening; auditable by construction, which removes the dependency on
`decision_evidence_links` — a substrate VT-725 was supposed to deliver and has not.

**What it gives up:** a 30-day-cycle wholesaler and a daily tiffin service get the same tiers.
That is genuinely worse than a reasoning Manager would do, and it is the accepted cost.

**The migration path is free** because of §Binding-semantics-8: the interval is read from policy.
When the Manager earns this, it supplies the value into the same socket, beneath a hard ceiling it
cannot widen. **CC's (a)/(b) distinction survives intact and should be preserved in the code
comments:** "how often should this business contact this customer" is a policy call that may one day
be reasoned; "should I re-send because a process crashed" is not a policy question at all and must
never become one.

## Dependencies / relationship to other rows

- **VT-288 / migration 071** — the link-tracking pattern to extend.

## Exit gate
(a) `read` is preserved distinctly from `delivered` end-to-end, proven by a real Twilio callback on
dev — not a unit test with a fabricated payload. (b) A tracked link in a customer message resolves,
records a click attributed to (tenant, customer, message), and redirects — proven by a real click.
(c) Reply attribution verified against the schema and proven, or its absence rostered. (d) All three
tiers computed correctly on seeded fixtures INCLUDING the boundary cases: a Tier-A customer is NOT
swallowed by B (the overlap correction, proven by assertion not inspection); a 31-day-old reply
drops A→B; a 91-day-old read drops B→C; zero-history → C; unreadable data → C. (e) Suppression
holds on ALL THREE re-drive paths, proven by forcing each. (f) DSR canary: purge a tenant, assert
click rows are gone — not merely FK-orphaned. (g) No real customer send; allowlist only.

## Boundaries
No customer send outside the allowlist. The rule never bends a correctness gate to permit a send.
No inference of engagement from delivery status. Prod is Fazal's.
