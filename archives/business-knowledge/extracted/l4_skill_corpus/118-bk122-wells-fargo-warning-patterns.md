## Situation

Wells Fargo used aggressive cross-sell goals while employees opened unauthorised accounts. Enforcement and board findings showed complaints, terminations and escalation failures before full consequences were accepted.

## Decision pressure

Leaders can call misconduct isolated because the headline metric is strong and cases are dispersed across complaints, refunds, terminations and locations.

## Mistake or risk

An ambitious target is not proof of misconduct, and firing frontline staff is not system remediation. The mechanism combines incentive design, warning aggregation, consent proof and leadership response.

## Recommended next action

Verify customer consent and beneficial use; aggregate complaints, reversals, refunds and conduct cases by leader/channel; test gaming paths; give Compliance escalation access; hold leaders accountable for pattern response.

## Evidence needed

- Customer consent and beneficial use
- Complaints, reversals and refunds
- Conduct cases by manager and pressure
- Gaming analysis and counter-metrics
- Escalation and leader response

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Sales and complaints rise together
- Many people are fired for one behaviour
- Controls report through Sales
- Consent is inferred from employee action

## Agent lesson

Sales owns customer value, Compliance owns assurance, Finance checks reversals, and the Manager treats dispersed warnings as system evidence.

## Hard-gate candidate

Incentivised customer actions require consent proof, outcome checks, pattern monitoring and independent escalation.
