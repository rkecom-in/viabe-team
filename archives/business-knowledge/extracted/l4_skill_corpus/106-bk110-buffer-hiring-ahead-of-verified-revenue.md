## Situation

Buffer's 2016 founder postmortem reports ten layoffs, about 11% of its team, after hiring and spending moved ahead of realised revenue growth.

## Decision pressure

Leaders fear delayed hiring will constrain growth, while an optimistic base case makes recurring headcount look affordable.

## Mistake or risk

A founder account is not independent proof, and hiring before demand is not always wrong. The failure mechanism is committing recurring cost without downside runway, forecast-error triggers, or staged release.

## Recommended next action

Approve hiring in tranches tied to realised revenue, margin, workload and runway; maintain a downside case; define a hiring-pause trigger before cash pressure forces abrupt layoffs; name the final cost owner.

## Evidence needed

- Realised versus forecast revenue and margin
- Loaded cost and time-to-productivity
- Base and downside runway
- Bottleneck evidence for the role
- Pause and correction triggers

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Hiring depends on the next fundraise
- Only the optimistic case funds payroll
- Headcount is treated as a growth metric
- No owner can pause hiring

## Agent lesson

Finance owns forecast and runway depth; the Manager releases fixed cost only when evidence and downside capacity support it.

## Hard-gate candidate

Material recurring hires require loaded cost, downside runway, realised-demand evidence and a pause trigger.

## Retrieval triggers

- hire ahead of growth
- headcount plan
- runway before hiring
- revenue forecast missed
- avoid layoffs
