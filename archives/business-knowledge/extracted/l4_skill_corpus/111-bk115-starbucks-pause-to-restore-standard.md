## Situation

As rapid expansion weakened its intended experience, Starbucks closed roughly 7,100 US company stores for several hours of espresso training for more than 135,000 people and separately closed weak locations.

## Decision pressure

Stopping revenue feels costlier than adding training around live operations even when staff cannot safely practise the standard there.

## Mistake or risk

Do not copy a visible closure when the root cause differs or treat training as the whole turnaround. The mechanism is protecting a critical standard with practice, verification and structural correction.

## Recommended next action

Identify critical behaviours, observe variance, pause throughput where needed for practice, certify performance, fix staffing/process/equipment causes, and monitor customer and operating outcomes after restart.

## Evidence needed

- Critical standard and variance
- Skill versus system root cause
- Pause cost versus continued harm
- Practice and certification method
- Post-restart quality and repeat measures

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Training substitutes for process repair
- Closure is publicity theatre
- No observed certification
- Growth resumes before standards recover

## Agent lesson

Specialists define customer and service measures; the Manager may accept a bounded revenue pause to restore a business-critical standard.

## Hard-gate candidate

A materially unreliable critical standard requires containment, root-cause correction and verified restart criteria.
