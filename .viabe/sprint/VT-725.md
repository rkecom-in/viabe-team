# VT-725 — connect the knowledge engine to the Manager

## RE-JUSTIFIED 2026-08-11 (Fazal) — this is a CONTROL PLANE, not a knowledge base

**Fazal, verbatim:** *"we must close the RAG implementation. That will help us tune luna's decision
on the fly and at tenant level. There will be times where luna or any model we are using may persist
on certain behaviour or decision which would need correction or tuning for that tenant."*

**This is a different and better justification than the one this row was written on**, and it
survives the measurements that killed the old one:

- The O11 null and the 88%-commodity result attacked **"cards make the Manager smarter."** They say
  nothing about **"cards let us steer one tenant's Manager at runtime."**
- It passes the moat gate (`ROLLING-QUEUE.md`). *Would a better model give this to a competitor for
  free?* **No.** A competitor on a better model still has no way to correct tenant-specific behaviour
  without a deploy. This is control-plane infrastructure — an **entry-ticket** justification, which
  the gate explicitly allows. It is not a knowledge claim.
- Without it, the only lever for "the Manager keeps doing the wrong thing for this tenant" is a code
  change and a deploy. That does not scale past a handful of tenants and it puts engineering in the
  path of every VTR observation.

### We already have TWO steering mechanisms. Do not build a third.
1. **O8 cards + per-tenant assignment** — the DELIBERATE lever. `manager_global | manager_tenant |
   specialist:<agent> | disabled`, evaluated per retrieval, changeable at runtime with no deploy.
   Fazal ratified it; **gate (b) already PROVED the per-tenant flip works.**
2. **VT-566 lessons read-back** — the AUTOMATIC lever. The owner's own edit/reject/approve verdicts
   (`agent_corrections`) rendered into the Manager's next run as "Lessons from this owner." **Built
   end-to-end and switched OFF** behind `MANAGER_MEMORY_RETRIEVAL` (default off).

They are complementary — automatic correction from owner behaviour, deliberate correction by a
VTR/admin — and **they must share ONE injection seam.** Two prompt-assembly paths will drift, which
is exactly the failure VT-718/720 fixed on the output side. Build the call site once; both sources
flow through it.

### THE DESIGN CONSEQUENCE — a tenant directive must be PINNED, never RANKED
This is the change that follows from the purpose changing, and it is the most important line in this
row.

**Measured recall is 0.229.** For *knowledge* retrieval, low recall is merely weak benefit. **For
steering, low recall is a broken control** — a VTR would correct a tenant's behaviour, watch it not
take effect, and have no way to tell whether the correction was wrong or simply never retrieved.

So: **a card assigned `manager_tenant` for a specific tenant is a DIRECTIVE. It is injected
deterministically — it does not compete in the ranking, it is not subject to the 0.250 floor, and it
is not displaced by top-k.** Semantic retrieval stays for the general corpus; directives bypass it
entirely. Anything else makes the steering lever probabilistic, and a probabilistic control is worse
than no control because it invites false confidence.

**Bounded:** a per-tenant directive budget (count and tokens), because pinned cards are cost on every
turn for that tenant (CL-2026-08-06-budget-aware-manager — caps are internal guardrails, never
invoices). Exceeding the budget is a VTR-visible condition, not a silent drop.

### THE BOUNDARY THAT MUST NOT BEND
**Steering is ADVISORY TO REASONING. It is not an effect gate, and it never becomes one.**
A directive that says "never discount for this tenant" *influences* the Manager; it does not
*prevent* the effect. The model may still ignore it. **If a behaviour genuinely must not happen for a
tenant, that belongs in a deterministic gate — not a card** (ARCHITECTURE §0.1.1; and
CL-2026-08-10: vetoes compose, permits do not).

Blur this and we will believe a tenant is protected when they are only advised. The steering plane
tunes preference; the gates hold the line.

2. **An undeclared identity retrieves NOTHING.** `retrieval_profile_for` and the new

3. **Attribution keys are stable across a replay.** Migration 183's uniqueness is
   `(tenant, run, decision, card, disposition)`, which is what makes a replayed DBOS step idempotent
   rather than a double-count in the ablation data. So the key is derived from the inbound message ref

## FLOOR RECALIBRATION — the derivation, recorded here as Clau required (2026-08-09)
Fazal authorized it verbatim: **"Recalibrate."** (relayed 2026-08-07T12:00Z). The requirement was
that the floor "remain a measured claim anyone can re-derive", so the method and the numbers live
here and in `apps/team-orchestrator/canaries/floor_calibration/` (scripts + `labels.json`).

**Why it was necessary.** The floor was `0.62`. Measured across **600 (case, card) pairs** on the
real 100-card dev corpus with real Voyage embeddings, the **single highest-scoring card scored
0.2867**. The bar sat 2.2x above the best attainable score — the corpus was inert by arithmetic, not
by any judgement about the knowledge in it. Gate (b) below could never have passed.

**Labels.** Every card in all 6 O11 cases labelled relevant/irrelevant **blind**: the labeller saw
the case's agent view only (never `acceptable_characteristics`, never the target answer), never a
score/rank/card-id, with card order shuffled per pass. 3 passes, majority vote.

| case | split | relevant | unanimous | pairwise agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev-compliance-gstr-readiness | dev | 10/100 | 95/100 | 0.58–0.83 |
| dev-finance-cashflow-wholesaler | dev | 11/100 | 96/100 | 0.69–0.83 |
| dev-sales-dormant-boutique | dev | 14/100 | 91/100 | 0.59–0.81 |
| val-d2c-returns-growth-tradeoff | val | 19/100 | 93/100 | 0.78–0.82 |
| val-restaurant-festival-capacity | val | 21/100 | 88/100 | 0.61–0.72 |
| val-tailor-price-increase | val | 10/100 | 89/100 | **0.50–0.53** |

**Distributions.** Pooled development **AUC = 0.753** (chance 0.5) — the scorer does discriminate.
Per case it runs 0.689–0.813 on dev and 0.599–0.686 on validation. The weakest AUC
(`val-tailor-price-increase`, 0.599) is also the weakest label agreement; those two cannot be
separated from this data and neither is being blamed.

**Chosen floor: 0.250.** Swept on DEVELOPMENT only; VALIDATION held out and never used to pick.
Precision measured on cards actually INJECTED (floor, then top_k), because injected cards are what
reach the Manager.

| floor | dev precision | val precision (held out) | cases retrieving |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.000 (no floor) | 0.375 | 0.174 | 3/3, 3/3 |
| 0.245 | 0.471 | — | 3/3 |
| **0.250** | **0.533** | **0.500–0.600** | 3/3 dev, 2/3–3/3 val |
| 0.255 | 0.538 | 0.500 | 3/3 dev, 2/3 val |
| 0.265 | 0.800 (n=5) | — | 2/3 dev |
| 0.290 | nothing retrieves | nothing retrieves | 0/3 |

The mandate was to bias the margin toward precision. Above 0.255 the apparent precision gain rests
on n<=5 while whole cases silently go dark — the exact failure the bias exists to prevent.

**Honest limits.** Recall at 0.250 is **0.229** — that is the RANKING, not the floor, and a floor
cannot promote a card the scorer ranked 40th. **9 of 35** dev relevant cards are hard-excluded by
applicability before scoring. Specialists inherit the Manager's number (their 0.58 was the same
unreachable guess); it is borrowed, flagged, and needs its own lane measurement.
**Voyage is non-deterministic** (+/-0.0019 semantic across identical runs), which is enough to flap
one held-out case whose best card sits at 0.2500–0.2505; that card is labelled irrelevant, so val
precision *rises* when it drops out.

**Coupling guard.** `card_retrieval.SCORE_WEIGHTS` is pinned by
`tests/orchestrator/knowledge/test_o8_floor_calibration.py`. A weight or metric change now fails
with a pointer to the re-derivation instead of leaving a floor fitted to a scale that no longer
exists — which is precisely how 0.62 rotted unnoticed.

## 2026-08-19 — the rc=1 triage, and the instrument defect it exposed

**Verdict on the `rc=1`: environment, not defect.** The 2026-08-17T23:27:58Z gates run failed with
three gate FAILs — "baseline: tenant A retrieved ZERO cards", "(d) the specialist retrieved nothing
at all", "(c) retrieval wrote ZERO decision_evidence_links rows" — and every one of them traced to a

was neither; it was an unmeasured run wearing a failed run's clothes.

**The instrument defect (fixed).** `card_serving` fail-softs to no-cards on ANY retrieval error, so a

proven — key absent → INDETERMINATE and no gates run; key present → PASS unchanged.

**The finding that outranks the triage.** Verifying the key's absence against the *deployment* rather

have caught this — it passes precisely because it runs with a key the deployment lacks. Key set on
