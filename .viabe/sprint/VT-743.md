# VT-743 — most blocks are "who is this?"

## Why this outranks the containment work it is rostered beside

VT-742 contains reputation damage. **This row prevents it.** The dominant cause of a WhatsApp block
or spam report is not message frequency — it is a customer receiving a message from a business they
do not recognise, cannot place, and cannot easily stop. Frequency governs how *often* an unrecognised
sender annoys someone; recognition governs whether they report it at all.

Every mitigation here is **inside our control and requires zero owner action**, which is the
constraint Fazal set when he ruled out owner-owned WABA as a launch dependency.

## Scope

### 1. First-contact identification — the highest-leverage single change
The first message a customer ever receives from a tenant must, in the customer's own language,
make three things unmistakable **before any offer**:
- **which business** this is (the tenant's actual trading name, not "Viabe")
- **why they are hearing from it** (the concrete basis — they bought, enquired, subscribed)
- **how to stop** (one action, stated plainly, in the same message)

A customer who can place the business does not report it. Today we send from a shared number the
customer has never seen, which makes recognition entirely dependent on the copy — so the copy is
load-bearing until VT-742 §1 lets a tenant send under their own identity.

### 2. Opt-out that is one action, honoured instantly, and never punished
The opt-out path exists and is authoritative (`pre_filter_gate`, `opt_out_keywords.yaml`,
Devanagari/Hinglish negation handling). **Audit it end-to-end from the CUSTOMER's side**, which is
the side never tested: is it discoverable, is it one action, is it acknowledged, does it apply
instantly across every path? Anything that makes stopping harder than reporting converts an opt-out
into a spam report — the worst possible exchange, because one is private and the other is scored.

### 3. Consent basis surfaced at send time, not just checked
Consent gates exist and hold. What does not exist is the **basis** travelling with the send — *why*
we believe this customer consented — so that a VTR investigating a complaint can answer "on what
grounds did we message this person" without reconstructing it. Record the basis on the send; surface
it in the diagnosis view (VT-742 §3 / VT-634's VTR surface).

### 4. Complaint/block as a first-class negative signal, fed back
VT-742 §5 captures the block. This row **closes the loop**: a block or opt-out is evidence about the
tenant's list hygiene, and a tenant whose blocks cluster on a specific import or cohort should be
surfaced to the VTR with that pattern named — not just a rate. The pattern is the actionable part;
a bare rate tells a VTR something is wrong and nothing about what.

## Boundaries
- **Zero owner action.** Same premise as VT-742.
- **Never weakens a correctness gate.** Consent, opt-out and Pillar-7 stay exactly as they are;
  this row makes them more legible, never more permissive.
- Copy is owner-language-aware (`en` / `hi` / hinglish free-form register per D1/D2). The welcome
  template stays en/hi only (CL-2026-08-10-welcome-is-en-hi-only) — that ruling covers the WELCOME
  template, not customer-facing campaign copy, which is per-cohort
  (`CampaignPlan.message_plan.language`) and must never be sourced from owner-locale columns.
- No real customer send; allowlist only.

## Exit gate
(a) First-contact copy carries business identity + basis + stop, proven rendered in each supported
language. (b) A customer opt-out sent as free text is honoured instantly across ALL send paths —
proven by forcing each path, not by reading the matcher. (c) Consent basis is recorded on the send
and visible in the VTR diagnosis view. (d) A clustered block pattern surfaces to the VTR naming the
cluster, not just a rate. (e) No correctness gate weakened; full-pack ×3 if the send path is touched.
