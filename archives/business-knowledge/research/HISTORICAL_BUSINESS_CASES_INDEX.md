# Historical business cases: growth, failure and recovery

Research date: 2026-07-26

## Admission rule

A case enters agent RAG only when the record supports: (1) a decision or operating mechanism, (2) an observable outcome, (3) a transferable action, and (4) a limit on the inference. A famous story, a founder quote, a single news allegation, or a post-hoc correlation alone is not enough. Primary filings, regulator postmortems and contemporaneous company records take precedence; reporting is used to fill chronology or operational detail and is labelled accordingly.

These are case-grounded patterns, not causal experiments. Agents must retrieve the mechanism and boundary—not recommend copying the named company.

## Accepted cases

### BK110 — Buffer: forecast-led hiring and forced correction (growth-stage)

- **Documented sequence:** Buffer's founder reported hiring and spending against a revenue plan, slower realised growth, and ten layoffs (about 11% of the team) in 2016.
- **Decision mechanism:** an optimistic forecast became recurring fixed cost without sufficiently early downside triggers.
- **Executional knowledge:** release hiring in tranches; calculate loaded cost and time-to-productivity; compare realised demand with base/downside runway; set a hiring-pause trigger and decision owner before pressure becomes acute.
- **Boundary:** first-party retrospective; it does not show that pre-demand hiring is always wrong.
- **Evidence:** [Buffer founder postmortem](https://buffer.com/resources/layoffs-and-moving-forward/). Locally archived.

### BK111 — Zerodha: constraint-led trust growth (bootstrapped Indian scale-up)

- **Documented approach:** Zerodha says it was bootstrapped and profitable early and avoided advertising, spam and metric-based growth targets in favour of customer-interest constraints and word of mouth.
- **Decision mechanism:** ownership/funding constraints, incentive design and product utility can reinforce trust-led acquisition.
- **Executional knowledge:** compare contribution, retention and referral by source; measure organic capacity; remove incentives that reward unsuitable action; use bounded paid tests where they add incremental profitable reach.
- **Boundary:** self-authored philosophy in regulated Indian brokerage; it does not prove that advertising, targets or external capital are generally harmful.
- **Evidence:** [Zerodha philosophy](https://zerodha.com/about/philosophy). Locally archived.

### BK112 — GoMechanic: management reporting integrity (Indian growth-stage failure)

- **Documented sequence:** investors said founders misstated facts including revenue and commissioned a third-party investigation; a founder acknowledged errors; the company announced a large workforce reduction and was later acquired by a consortium.
- **Decision mechanism:** fundraising pressure and weak separation between growth ownership, financial reporting and independent assurance.
- **Executional knowledge:** define recognition/cut-off; reconcile contract → delivery → invoice → collection → bank/GST/statutory records; investigate gaps before financing; give Finance and Compliance direct escalation rights.
- **Boundary:** do not infer fraud from ordinary variance or use media accounts for a legal conclusion.
- **Evidence:** [investor statement and founder response reported by Reuters/ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/investors-of-indias-gomechanic-seek-audit-into-inflated-financials/1322954/) and [subsequent acquisition reporting](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/28/distressed-indian-startup-gomechanic-acquired-by-lifelong-group-led-consortium/). Live links only.

### BK113 — Good Glamm: acquisition pace versus integration capacity (Indian scale-up)

- **Documented sequence:** the company assembled eleven acquisitions; its founder later described moving too much, too fast and too big, while reporting documented restructuring, delayed payments and asset-sale discussions.
- **Decision mechanism:** deal momentum outran integration capacity and cash resilience, with the model exposed to future financing.
- **Executional knowledge:** model standalone and integration cash; name an integration owner; cap concurrent integrations; use 30/90/180-day synergy, customer, data and cash gates; stop new deals when prior gates miss.
- **Boundary:** later distress has multiple causes; this does not show acquisitions or house-of-brands strategies always fail.
- **Evidence:** [Business Standard founder account](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/beauty-unicorn-ceo-blames-strategy-collapse-125080101574_1.html) and [Moneycontrol restructuring interview](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/several-restructuring-discussions-underway-with-lenders-good-glamm-s-darpan-sanghvi-13232070.html). Live links only.

### BK114 — Domino's: product truth before promotion (large consumer turnaround)

- **Documented sequence:** Domino's paired public acknowledgement of customer criticism with a recipe change, advertising and operating execution; its 2010 annual report reported strong US same-store sales.
- **Decision mechanism:** the campaign made a verifiable product change salient and invited lapsed customers to retry; it was not message-only reputation repair.
- **Executional knowledge:** structure criticism; test the fix; verify delivery consistency; communicate the old problem and changed proof; measure trial, repeat, complaints and cohort economics rather than reach alone.
- **Boundary:** bundled intervention and company attribution, not an experiment isolating advertising causality.
- **Evidence:** [Domino's 2010 annual report](https://ir.dominos.com/static-files/9a709686-fd64-451a-86b5-8ce23511a49a). Locally archived.

### BK115 — Starbucks: pause throughput to restore a service standard (large turnaround)

- **Documented sequence:** Starbucks closed about 7,100 US company-operated stores for several hours to retrain more than 135,000 people on espresso preparation, alongside wider turnaround actions and weak-store closures.
- **Decision mechanism:** the company accepted a bounded revenue interruption to make a critical customer-facing standard observable and practisable.
- **Executional knowledge:** identify critical behaviours; distinguish skill from staffing/process/equipment causes; practise outside live throughput where required; certify; fix structural causes; monitor post-restart quality, speed, complaints and repeat.
- **Boundary:** a theatrical closure without verified competence or structural repair is not the mechanism.
- **Evidence:** [Starbucks transformation memo](https://archive.starbucks.com/uploads/2018/12/4f-transformation-memos-transformation-agenda-communication-6-2008-feb-11-final.pdf). Live link only; host denied automated archival download.

### BK116 — Adobe: deliberate model cannibalisation (large technology transition)

- **Documented sequence:** Adobe moved creative products from perpetual licences to Creative Cloud subscriptions, disclosed the near-term revenue/accounting transition and tracked subscription measures; by FY2015 it described perpetual creative revenue as immaterial.
- **Decision mechanism:** management judged the transition with recurring/cohort measures rather than defending legacy upfront revenue.
- **Executional knowledge:** bridge accounting revenue to cash; compare old/new cohort contribution; measure conversion, activation, usage, retention and churn; stage migration and protect support/runway; explicitly authorise useful cannibalisation.
- **Boundary:** subscription is unsuitable without recurring customer value; Adobe's brand and product conditions do not transfer automatically.
- **Evidence:** [Adobe FY2012 filing](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/cc/en/investor-relations/pdfs/ADBE-10K-FY12-FINAL.pdf) and [Adobe FY2015 filing](https://www.adobe.com/content/dam/cc/en/investor-relations/pdfs/ADBE-10K-FY15-FINAL.pdf). Both locally archived.

### BK117 — Netflix/Qwikster: correct destination, harmful migration (large digital business)

- **Documented sequence:** Netflix pursued streaming but combined price and service/brand separation in 2011; Qwikster became a symbol of not listening, domestic net additions turned negative, and the separation was reversed.
- **Decision mechanism:** strategically rational internal separation created customer discontinuity in account, brand, price and workflow.
- **Executional knowledge:** assess destination separately from migration; map discontinuities; test painful components separately; stage cohorts; monitor cancellation reasons/support load; preserve component-level rollback.
- **Boundary:** subscriber movement had multiple causes; backlash against migration does not by itself invalidate the streaming strategy.
- **Evidence:** [Netflix Q4 2011 investor letter](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/quarterly_reports/2011/q4/Investor-Letter-Q4-2011.pdf) and [Q3 2011 transcript](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/quarterly_reports/2011/q3/NFLX-Transcript-2011-10-24T22_00.pdf). Investor letter locally archived.

### BK118 — Target Canada: scale before end-to-end readiness (large international failure)

- **Documented sequence:** Target opened 133 Canadian stores rapidly while item data, supply-chain flow, training, availability, price and assortment problems persisted; it exited after deciding profitability was not realistic until at least 2021 and estimated a US$5.4 billion pretax discontinued-operations loss.
- **Decision mechanism:** lease and schedule commitments overrode a representative end-to-end readiness proof; aggregate inventory hid shelf-level failure and local proposition mismatch.
- **Executional knowledge:** canary a real item/customer flow through setup, supplier, receipt, replenishment, shelf, price, purchase and return; test exception cycles; require data accuracy, availability, customer-fit and unit-economics gates before each wave.
- **Boundary:** multi-cause failure; reducing it to software or supply chain alone would be false precision.
- **Evidence:** [Target exit announcement](https://corporate.target.com/press/release/2015/01/target-corporation-announces-plans-to-discontinue-canadian-operations), [2014 annual report](https://corporate.target.com/getmedia/ce16ceb0-3600-46c0-9dc5-47b303250fff/Target-2014-Annual-Report.pdf), and [Reuters operational reconstruction](https://m.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/thin-crowds-during-ceo%27s-visit-helped-seal-target-canada%27s-demise-323916). Annual report locally archived.

### BK119 — WeWork: duration mismatch and governance (large growth failure)

- **Documented sequence:** WeWork used long lease commitments to serve comparatively flexible memberships, accumulated losses and disclosed related-party dealings; its 2019 IPO failed and it entered bankruptcy in 2023.
- **Decision mechanism:** short/cancellable revenue funded rigid long obligations while rapid footprint growth obscured mature-unit and downside-liquidity economics; governance conflicts weakened trust.
- **Executional knowledge:** map revenue/obligation duration and repricing; stress occupancy/churn; cap expansion by mature-unit payback and downside liquidity; record related parties and require independent approval.
- **Boundary:** it does not prove coworking is inherently unviable, and bankruptcy cannot be reduced to one ratio.
- **Evidence:** [WeWork 2019 S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1533523/000162827919000125/filename1.htm) and [AP bankruptcy account](https://apnews.com/article/be8c36b9720377334645e37c73d55e6e). Live links only; SEC denied automated archival download.

### BK120 — Silicon Valley Bank: concentration, duration and correlated liquidity (large regulated failure)

- **Documented sequence:** the Federal Reserve review found rapid growth, concentrated largely uninsured deposits, inadequate interest-rate and liquidity management, and risk assumptions changed in ways that reduced measured exposure before an unusually fast coordinated run.
- **Decision mechanism:** concentrated actors behaved together while callable funding supported longer-duration exposure; risk capacity lagged growth.
- **Executional knowledge:** map customer/supplier/lender/bank concentration and correlated groups; time inflows against fixed outflows; run severe sudden-loss/delay scenarios; diversify backups; set activation triggers and authority.
- **Boundary:** SMB agents should transfer the logic, not bank-specific ratios or regulatory rules.
- **Evidence:** [Federal Reserve SVB review](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/review-of-the-federal-reserves-supervision-and-regulation-of-silicon-valley-bank.htm). Locally archived.

### BK121 — Southwest: recovery capacity is part of product capacity (large operational failure)

- **Documented sequence:** winter weather triggered Southwest's December 2022 disruption, but scheduling recovery, notification, refunds and reimbursements failed at scale; US DOT action concerned more than two million passengers and a US$140 million settlement.
- **Decision mechanism:** normal-throughput optimisation underfunded degraded-mode recovery, backlog clearance, communication and remedy capacity.
- **Executional knowledge:** size backlog creation versus recovery throughput; exercise degraded/manual modes; reserve communication and remedy authority; test until the backlog clears—not merely until systems restart.
- **Boundary:** separate the external trigger, technology, process, staffing and customer-remedy failures; “legacy technology” alone is not an adequate diagnosis.
- **Evidence:** [US DOT enforcement summary](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/dot-penalizes-southwest-airlines-140-million-2022-holiday-meltdown) and [consent order](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/southwest-order-2023-12-11). Live links only; host denied automated archival download.

### BK122 — Wells Fargo: incentive harm and ignored warning patterns (large compliance failure)

- **Documented sequence:** aggressive cross-sell goals coexisted with unauthorised customer accounts; enforcement and independent board findings identified complaints, repeated employee terminations and escalation/leadership failures before the system-level problem was accepted.
- **Decision mechanism:** outcome incentives rewarded activity that could be produced without consent, while dispersed warnings were treated as individual misconduct rather than a pattern.
- **Executional knowledge:** verify consent and beneficial use; aggregate complaints, reversals, refunds and conduct cases by manager/channel; model gaming paths; ensure Compliance independence; hold leaders responsible for pattern response.
- **Boundary:** an ambitious target is not proof of misconduct, and removing/firing frontline staff alone is not remediation.
- **Evidence:** [CFPB enforcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-fines-wells-fargo-100-million-widespread-illegal-practice-secretly-opening-unauthorized-accounts/) and [Wells Fargo board findings](https://newsroom.wf.com/news-releases/news-details/2017/Wells-Fargo-Board-Releases-Findings-of-Independent-Investigation-of-Retail-Banking-Sales-Practices-and-Related-Matters/default.aspx). CFPB page locally archived.

## Cases deliberately not admitted

- **Generic “company X failed because it did not innovate” summaries:** mechanism and counterfactual are too ambiguous.
- **Theranos and Volkswagen as extra cards:** valuable evidence exists, but the current corpus already covers independent verification, incentive/governance failure and compliance escalation. Another famous misconduct story would add recall more than new executional knowledge.
- **Single-source claims about founder personality or culture:** not admitted without a traceable operating mechanism and corroborated outcome.
- **MarketResearch catalogue listings and paywalled abstracts:** useful for discovery, not RAG evidence unless the underlying report and methods are available.
