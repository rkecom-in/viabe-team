# Specialist Executional Research Index — Round 5

This index records the evidence selected for specialist-agent execution and COO governance, together with local originals and access boundaries. Findings are decision aids, not universal effect-size promises. Mutable law, regulation, platform policy, and cyber-reporting requirements must be verified at action time.

## Marketing and Sales

### Recipient-benefiting customer referrals

- Source: HBS/Journal of Marketing Research, *Why Prosocial Referral Incentives Work: The Interplay of Reputational Benefits and Action Costs*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=GershonCryderJohn+-+Why+Prosocial+Incentives+Work.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/GershonCryderJohn - Why Prosocial Incentives Work_3a65737a-0749-4008-86f6-70aa9945db97.pdf`
- Method: two field studies, one laboratory study, and five controlled experiments.
- Executional learning: a reward for the referred customer can outperform an equally valued reward for the sender because it improves the referrer's social signal and reduces the recipient's action cost. Diagnose whether the bottleneck is willingness to refer or recipient uptake; test sender, recipient, shared, and non-cash variants using activated contribution rather than referral volume.
- Transfer boundary: consumer settings and specific incentive sizes do not establish a universal uplift; economics, consent, tax, anti-spam, and platform rules require local checks.
- RAG: BK094.

### Learn-then-earn price experiments

- Source: HBS working paper, *Demand Learning and Pricing for Varying Assortments*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=Demand+Learning+and+Pricing+for+Varying+Assortments.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/Demand Learning and Pricing for Varying Assortments_d1e6413b-cfb4-4a7a-94d7-0d8534287f20.pdf`
- Method: attribute-level demand estimation, pricing algorithm, and field experiment with fitness studios.
- Executional learning: a bounded exploration phase can sacrifice some short-term revenue to learn price response, then exploit the learning across changing assortments. Predeclare the learning budget, price bounds, customer-fairness rules, margin floor, and stop conditions.
- Transfer boundary: the reported monthly effects depend on the marketplace, inventory, and algorithm; they are not SMB targets.
- RAG: BK095.

### Predicting promotion incrementality from prior experiments

- Source: HBS Working Paper 24-076, *Incrementality Prediction: Synergizing Past Experiments for Intervention Personalization*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=24-076.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/24-076_c3424b9b-adbb-4aa9-897f-c29ba27687aa.pdf`
- Method: 362 randomized promotion experiments covering more than 7.5 million customers and 30 million offer-customer observations.
- Executional learning: a consistent library of randomized experiments can help predict heterogeneous incremental response to a new offer. Re-run a randomized test when the customer, channel, product, objective, or offer concept has shifted materially.
- Transfer boundary: this approach is inappropriate when experiment history is sparse, definitions drift, interference is material, or targeting would violate privacy/fairness constraints.
- RAG: BK096.

### Search friction and customer transparency

- Source: HBS Working Paper 19-080, *The Impact of Increasing Search Frictions on Online Shopping Behavior: Evidence from a Field Experiment*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=19-080.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/19-080_aae30b91-4631-422c-b843-b55e7db9e3ae.pdf`
- Method: retailer field experiments altering outlet links, discount sorting, and discount markers.
- Executional learning: interface findability changes both conversion and price mix, so an apparent revenue win can be a transparency, trust, complaint, or retention loss. Test comprehension, findability, returns, complaints, and repeat behavior—not only basket value.
- Transfer boundary: never hide material prices, terms, eligibility, or safer alternatives; obtain current consumer-law and platform review.
- RAG: BK097.

### Long-term targeting with short-term signals

- Source: HBS working paper, *Doing More with Less: Overcoming Ineffective Long-term Targeting Using Short-Term Signals*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=23-023.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/23-023_5b02c937-1c15-42ea-9a95-ae7b6f17fd21.pdf`
- Method: theory, simulation, and a food-technology coupon experiment.
- Executional learning: directly fitting noisy long-term outcomes can target worse than a simple policy. A prevalidated short-term surrogate combined with separate frequency/churn estimation can be more useful, but must be monitored for surrogate drift and downstream harm.
- Transfer boundary: a short-term metric is not automatically a valid surrogate; require historical validation against contribution and retention.
- RAG: BK098.

### Supplier service reliability and retailer demand

- Source: HBS Working Paper 11-034, *The Impact of Supplier Inventory Service Level on Retailer Demand*.
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=11-034.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/hbs/11-034_95f981a4-388b-40f3-9751-fb654b05162e.pdf`
- Method: longitudinal supplier-retailer-SKU order data plus a service-improvement pilot in branded apparel.
- Executional learning: fill rate, stockouts, recovery time, and order reliability can influence future channel demand; price concessions should not conceal a service-reliability problem.
- Transfer boundary: the estimated relationship is supplier- and category-specific and is not a universal causal elasticity.
- RAG: BK101.

## People and Operations

### Employee referrals: quantity, quality, and retention

- Source: NBER Working Paper 25920, *The Effects of Employee Referral Programs on Employee Retention and Productivity*.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w25920
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/management/nber_employee_referrals_w25920.pdf`
- Method: randomized introduction and bonus variation in a grocery chain.
- Executional learning: larger bonuses increased referral quantity but reduced average quality; the programme reduced attrition partly through effects on non-referred staff. Optimize retained productive hires and team effects, not referrals alone.
- Transfer boundary: labour market, store performance, role, and referral-network homogeneity affect transfer.
- RAG: BK099.

### Indian supply-chain relationship resilience

- Source: NBER Working Paper 30689, *Supply Chain Resilience: Evidence from Indian Firms*.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w30689
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/operations/nber_india_supply_chain_resilience_w30689.pdf`
- Method: event study around COVID-19 lockdowns using a universe of firm-to-firm transactions in an Indian state.
- Executional learning: map dependency by input criticality, substitutability, supplier concentration, geography, and recovery time. Preserve hard-to-replace relationships while prequalifying fallback suppliers for substitutable inputs.
- Transfer boundary: the lockdown was an extreme shock and the observed state/period is not every Indian supply chain.
- RAG: BK100.

## Finance and Resilience

### Trade credit as an operating liquidity buffer

- Source: NBER Working Paper 22286, *Trade Credit and Liquidity Provision*.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w22286
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/finance/nber_trade_credit_liquidity_w22286.pdf`
- Method: exogenous liquidity losses following cash-in-transit fraud/failure.
- Executional learning: firms can respond to liquidity shocks by drawing more supplier credit and tightening customer credit. Model both directions with supplier/customer concentration, relationship cost, collection behaviour, and cash runway.
- Transfer boundary: shifting liquidity can transmit distress and damage counterparties; it is not free financing.
- RAG: BK102.

### Records and relationship lending

- Source: NBER Working Paper 8752, *Small Business Credit Availability and Relationship Lending*.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w8752
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/finance/nber_small_bank_soft_information_w8752.pdf`
- Method: empirical comparison of large- and small-bank interactions with small firms.
- Executional learning: maintain a standardized lender pack while deliberately building a relationship with an institution capable of using verifiable soft information. Relationship evidence complements—not replaces—clean records.
- Transfer boundary: US banking institutions and the study period differ from Indian underwriting, digital cash-flow data, guarantees, and current regulation.
- RAG: BK103.

### Payment fallback and reconciliation

- Source: NBER Working Paper 35115, *Payment Resilience*.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35115
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/finance/nber_payment_resilience_w35115.pdf`
- Method: outage event studies, surveys in three countries, and a randomized intervention.
- Executional learning: preserve alternate acquirer/bank/rail options, an offline or cash fallback where lawful and safe, staff instructions, customer communication, and post-outage reconciliation.
- Transfer boundary: payment rails and cash behaviour differ by country; validate Indian provider capabilities, RBI rules, fraud exposure, and operational constraints.
- RAG: BK104.

## Compliance, Fraud, and Cyber Operations

### India MSME cyber-control baseline

- Source: CERT-In, *15 Elemental Cyber Defense Controls for MSMEs*, version 1.0 (2025).
- URL: https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/Elemental_Cyber_Defense_Controls_for_MSME.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/certin_15_msme_cyber_controls.pdf`
- Method: Indian national incident-response authority guidance with 15 controls and 45 recommendations.
- Executional learning: establish asset inventory, licensed protection, MFA, secure configuration and patching, access reviews, third-party checks, offline/offsite tested backups, logging, training, vulnerability assessment, incident exercises, and a named security owner.
- Mutable requirements: verify current CERT-In Directions, reporting timelines, log-retention/location requirements, sector rules, and DPDP obligations at action time.
- RAG: BK105 and BK106.

### SMB cyber-risk prioritization

- Source: NIST, *Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Small Business Quick-Start Guide*.
- URL: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.1300
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/nist_csf20_small_business_quick_start.pdf`
- Executional learning: connect Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover activities to critical business services and third-party dependencies rather than treating cyber as a tool-buying checklist.
- Transfer boundary: this is a US voluntary framework; Indian law, CERT-In directions, and sector controls override where applicable.
- RAG: BK105 and BK106.

### Business-email and invoice-redirection fraud

- Sources: FBI IC3 public service announcements and the 2025 IC3 Annual Report.
- URLs: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2016/PSA160614 and https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2023/PSA230324
- Archived originals: `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/ic3_bec_scenarios_2016.html`, `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/ic3_vendor_invoice_fraud_2023.html`, and `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/ic3_2025_annual_report.pdf`.
- Executional learning: never approve bank-detail or beneficiary changes on the requesting channel alone; independently call a known number, inspect the domain, use dual approval, create a cooling period for changed beneficiaries, and rehearse rapid bank/incident escalation.
- Transfer boundary: IC3 losses are reported US cases and must not be presented as Indian prevalence estimates.
- RAG: BK107.

### Competition compliance for sales, bids, and associations

- Source: Competition Commission of India, *Competition Advocacy Booklet 2026*.
- URL: https://www.cci.gov.in/images/publications_booklet/en/competition-advocacy-booklet-2026-english1779277830.pdf
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/compliance/cci_competition_advocacy_2026.pdf`
- Executional learning: define competitor-contact rules; review bid, distribution, pricing, exclusivity, tying, and association activities; keep agreement and meeting records; provide an advice/escalation channel; train Sales, Marketing, and Procurement using practical scenarios.
- Mutable requirements: the card is an issue-spotting and escalation protocol, not a legal conclusion. Obtain current Indian competition-law advice for the actual facts.
- RAG: BK108.

## India SMB Benchmarking

### India Micro Enterprise Survey 2022

- Source: World Bank Enterprise Surveys, India Micro Enterprise Survey 2022.
- Catalog: https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/6495
- Archived documentation: `archives/business-knowledge/research/datasets/world_bank_india_micro_enterprise_questionnaire_2022.pdf`, `archives/business-knowledge/research/datasets/world_bank_india_micro_enterprise_implementation_2022.pdf`, `archives/business-knowledge/research/datasets/world_bank_india_micro_enterprise_metadata_2022.json`, and the 140-page DDI export `archives/business-knowledge/research/datasets/ddi-documentation-english_microdata-6495.pdf`.
- Coverage: 998 formally registered firms with fewer than five employees across nine Indian cities; 237 variables across infrastructure, sales and supplies, management practices, competition, permits, crime, finance, government relations, labour, environment, and performance.
- Executional learning: match definitions, geography, size class, sector, fiscal period, and survey weights before treating a number as a benchmark. Raw case counts are not population prevalence.
- Access boundary: the raw microdata is distributed via the World Bank Enterprise Surveys access process; this archive does not bypass registration or licence terms.
- RAG: BK109.

### India Enterprise Survey 2022 country profile

- Source: World Bank Enterprise Surveys, India 2022 country profile.
- Archived original: `archives/business-knowledge/research/datasets/world_bank_india_enterprise_survey_2022.pdf`
- Executional learning: use formal-enterprise benchmarks as context, then segment by size, sector, region, and survey design before comparing an individual SMB.
- Transfer boundary: country averages can conceal state, industry, informality, age, and size differences.
- RAG: BK109.
