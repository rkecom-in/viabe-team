## Situation

Zerodha describes a profitable, bootstrapped operating philosophy centred on product utility, customer-interest constraints and word of mouth rather than advertising and metric-based growth targets.

## Decision pressure

Competitors' paid acquisition and sales targets make visible growth activity tempting even when it may weaken trust or unit economics.

## Mistake or risk

This is self-reported evidence from a regulated brokerage with distinctive conditions. It does not prove that advertising, targets or outside capital are inherently harmful.

## Recommended next action

Test contribution, retention, referral propensity and organic capacity; remove incentives that reward unsuitable customer actions; run bounded paid experiments only where incremental profitable reach preserves the promise.

## Evidence needed

- Contribution and retention by source
- Referral rate and customer reasons
- Customer-harm risk from incentives
- Organic capacity versus opportunity window
- Incremental paid-channel lift

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Organic growth is praised but unmeasured
- Trust excuses a weak product
- Incentives oppose customer interest
- A no-advertising philosophy is copied without category evidence

## Agent lesson

Sales and Marketing design channels, Finance validates economics, and the Manager protects customer-interest boundaries without copying founder philosophy as universal law.

## Hard-gate candidate

A channel philosophy requires contribution, retention, referral, capacity and customer-harm evidence before becoming policy.
