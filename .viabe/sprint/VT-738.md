# VT-738 — the route decision does not survive a repeat

## The defect, stated precisely (and NOT as a single rate)

**Proven:** `sr_second_plan_queue_busy`, run ×3 in the 79-scenario gate, **identical seed
(8 customers), identical owner message** → `route` diverged `['none', 'sales_recovery', 'none']`.
The one successful run grounded all 8 customers and correctly reached `paused`. Same input, three
runs, two different answers. That is the defect and it needs no rate to be real.

**The blast radius is known and it is narrow-but-critical:** all **16 blocks** in the ×3 gate came
from **6 distinct scenarios**, and all 6 share this one root cause —
`second_plan_queue_busy` 3/3 · `sr_l2_owner_approval_gates_send` 2/3 ·
`sr_second_plan_queue_busy` 2/3 · `sr_owner_cannot_bypass_approval_by_asking` 1/3 ·
`routing_db_proof_finance_vs_sr` 1/3 · `plan_queue_status_check_while_pending` 1/3.

## Correcting the number this row will be quoted with

`GO-LIVE-BLOCKERS.md` B1 and the 2026-08-09T15:00Z brief both say **"the Manager declines ~1 task
in 3"**, sourced from *"34 delegating turns vs 11 honest-failure replies."* **I retracted that rate
in the promotion package on 2026-08-06 and it should not be re-quoted.** Three reasons:

1. **The arithmetic does not give 33%.** 11 / (34+11) = **24%**, not a third.
2. **"Honest-failure reply" is not a synonym for "missed delegation."** An honest failure can be
   *correct* behaviour — the Manager declining something genuinely out of lane. Claude Code declines
   tasks too; that is not the defect. Conflating the two inflates the miss and, worse, would let a
   "fix" that suppresses honest failures look like progress.
3. **The sample is not the Manager's whole surface.** The 34/11 was counted inside an SR-heavy
   alphabetical cluster. I already corrected myself twice on this number — first "~1-in-3"
   (understated), then "~40%" (overstated, same clustering bug).

**The honest statement is the divergence, not a rate.** Deriving a trustworthy miss rate is
in scope for this row (see exit gate) — but it must be measured on a defined denominator over the
SR pack, not inherited from the retracted figure.

## ROOT CAUSE — investigated 2026-08-09/10. The premise was wrong; here is what is actually true.

Twenty-agent fan-out over the routing path, every causal claim put through adversarial refutation,
then **every load-bearing number re-derived by me from `reports/vt734_critical_x3.json`** rather
than accepted. Nine investigator claims were discarded as unsafe; they are listed at the end.

### The measured numbers (recomputed, re-runnable)

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "34 delegating vs 11 honest-failure" | `34` = `[internal route: sales_recovery]` markers over **552 steps**; `11` = replies containing `"couldn't complete it on my own"`. **Not complements. No shared denominator.** |
| "~1 turn in 3" / 24% / 40% | On the pack's own denominator — steps declaring `assert_route.expect_sr_delegation: true` — **3 misses / 33 = 9.1%**, of which only **2 (6.1%) are a real delegation failure**. |

| "identical seed, identical message → `['none','sales_recovery','none']`" | That array is `sr_second_plan_queue_busy`'s **last step only. Step 0 was identical 3/3** — same escalated closure, route `none`. There is no step-0 non-determinism, and the diverging step's correct behaviour is *not* to create a campaign. |

**The 11 "honest failures" are POST-delegation.** `owner_surface/task_outcome.py:249,266` emits that
copy only for `outcome == "escalated"`, which is only ever armed against a `task_id`
(`manager/workflow.py:466,1033,1281`). A task existed ⇒ **the Manager delegated.** Counting them as
the failure side of "34 vs 11" asserts that a delegation which then blocked is a delegation that
never happened.

**I opened all 5 misses. Not one is a Manager that declined.** 1 measurement race, 2 post-delegation
escalations, 2 async-never-landed.

### The transcript marker is a stale snapshot — do not count misses from it

The fan-out reported **5/33 = 15.2%**, counted off the `[internal route: …]` transcript marker, and
I initially confirmed that number. **It is an overcount and I retract it.** Two of those five
(`sr_l2_owner_approval_gates_send [2/3]`, `sr_no_actual_send_before_approval [1/3]`) are labelled
`PASS` with zero failures, and reading their FULL transcripts shows why: the marker is written the
instant the run leaves `running`, but SR delegation routinely completes after that — **the step-0
draft appears in the step-1 transcript.** The harness's own `assert_route` re-reads later and
correctly sees the campaign. Those two delegated; they were just slow.

So the harness label is the more honest signal and the marker is the stale one — **the opposite of
what I said in my first pass on this row.** The three real failures:

| miss | what actually happened | class |
|---|---|---|
| `sr_l2_owner_approval_gates_send [1/3]` step0 | *"I couldn't complete it on my own"*, no campaign ever | **M1 — real** |
| `sr_owner_cannot_bypass_approval_by_asking [1/3]` step0 | same closure, no campaign ever | **M1 — real** |
| `routing_db_proof_finance_vs_sr [1/3]` step1 | the 6-customer draft in the transcript belongs to **step 0's** run; step 1 was asked to plan again and only acked | **attribution / scenario expectation** |

**The honest headline: 2 genuine delegation failures in 33 asserted turns = 6.1%**, plus one turn
whose expectation needs re-examining. Not 33%, not 24%, not 15.2%.

**And both genuine misses are Hinglish D3-ineligible phrasings** — *"…offer draft kar do"* and
*"…win-back message taiyar karo"*. The same scenario that failed in run 1 **succeeded in run 2**.
That is M4, and it is the whole of the non-determinism: turns the frozen keyword net cannot reach
fall to the LLM classifier, and the classifier is not stable across runs.

### The named mechanisms, ranked

**M1 — the enforce loop defaults a no-spawn dispatch to `escalate`, silently.** `manager/workflow.py:342`:
```python
outcome = "paused_approval" if is_paused else str(terminal_state.get("manager_review_outcome") or "escalate")
```
In enforce mode `manager_review` is edged **only** from specialist nodes (`supervisor.py:822-824`),
and `orchestrator_terminal → END` is unconditional (`:875`). So a dispatch that spawns nothing never
reaches `manager_review`, `manager_review_outcome` is absent, and `or "escalate"` fires →
`_arm_escalation_for_notify` (`:1281`) → the exact observed copy. **The escalate branch's own comment
("manager_review already settled the task blocked") is false on this path** — nothing ran, no
incident, no audit row. *Verified by me at the cited lines.*

**M2 — the route marker is a `campaigns`-row-existence proxy, read before an async write.**
`canaries/convo_harness.py:515-526` returns `sales_recovery` iff a `campaigns` row joins to the turn,
else `none`. Specialist `insufficient_data`, `CohortRejectedError` rollback, approval-budget
exhaustion, a late draft, and genuine non-delegation **all collapse to the same `none`.**
`convo_harness.py:1828` gates settle-polling on `assert_side_effects`/`assert_grounded_count` —
**`assert_route` is not in that list**, so an assert_route-only step never re-polls.
`routing_db_proof_finance_vs_sr [1/3] step1` is the proof: marker `none`, then a 6-customer draft and
an approval ask in the same transcript. **1 of the 5 is purely this.**

**M3 — the step-1 divergence is wedged state, not sampling.** Step 0 escalates → task `blocked`;
`manager/task_store.py:44` keeps `blocked` in `TASK_ACTIVE`, so it **holds the tenant's slot** ⇒
`triage_seam.py:508 has_active_task=True` ⇒ D3 (`:583`) and the LLM route (`:640`) both skipped ⇒
plan admitted `queued` ⇒ `:694` fails ⇒ **the legacy sync brain owns the turn.** Runs 1 and 2 *both*
delegated; `route` cannot tell them apart (run 1 hit `CampaignPlanInsufficientData`, run 2 drafted 8
and paused). `route='sales_recovery'` there is the *legacy-fallback* signature, not the healthy one.

**M4 — 14 of 21 SR-asserted turns are structurally outside the deterministic router.**
`onboarding/campaign_first_contact.py:74-84` requires **VERB ∧ NOUN**. Hinglish phrasings
(`"…taiyar karo"`, `"…plan bana do"`, `"kuch karo unhe wapas laane ke liye"`) fail it, fall to
`triage_turn`, and route only if the LLM emits `task_kind == "campaign_recovery"`. **D3-eligible
6/6 = 100%; D3-ineligible 22/27 = 81%; all 5 misses are D3-ineligible.** This is the one place
where model variance genuinely owns the route — and it is confined to phrasings the frozen keyword
net cannot reach. Which is exactly the shape [[no-lists-for-undefined-possibilities]] warns about:
a hardcoded keyword list standing in for natural-language intent.

### Ruled OUT, with reasons (so nobody re-opens them)
- **Knowledge injection / the new floor / Fast budget** — ruled out on timeline (the gate ran
  2026-08-05; those landed 2026-08-09) *and* on reachability: `retrieve_cards_for_turn` has **no
  runtime caller** and `INJECTS_INTO_PROMPT` is `ClassVar[bool] = False`.
- **Temperature** — nothing is pinned, but the deployed gpt-5.6 family *rejects* the parameter. A
  property, not a fault, and it has zero discriminating power (it explains the 8 always-delegating
  scenarios equally well).
- **`run_critical_x3.py:180` dropping `turn_sid`** — real, but a false-*negative* generator: all 5/177
  mismatches run `summary=none` vs `marker=sales_recovery`. It *suppresses* divergence; it cannot
  manufacture the quoted array (both keyings agree exactly for that scenario). Gate honesty, not
  this defect.
- **`convo_harness.py:1846` dropping `message_sid`** — latent, **zero readers today**.

### Why nobody could name it: the loop is structurally unobservable
1. **The loop's route is recorded nowhere.** `manager/task_producer.py:63` returns early on
   `route_key == "terminal"`, and `:70` returns early when `manager_task_id is not None` — **both
   apply to every enforce-loop dispatch.** No row anywhere says whether the Manager emitted a spawn.
2. **`_arm_escalation_for_notify` writes no audit row and no incident.** M1 is silent by construction.
3. **The loop's node trace is dead — and it STAYS dead for now, deliberately.**
   `workflow.py` opens `observability_context(run_id=UUID(task_id))`, but
   `migrations/006_pipeline_steps.sql:24` is `run_id UUID NOT NULL REFERENCES pipeline_runs (id)`
   and `manager_tasks.id` has no such row — while `_open_dispatch_run` created the row under a
   *different* id. Every `write_step` FK-violates and is swallowed at
   `observability/langgraph_hooks.py`. **The richest existing discriminator is off for the entire
   enforce loop.** *Verified: FK and both call sites read at the cited lines.*

   **I built the one-token fix, then reverted it.** The fan-out proposed opening the context under
   `run_id` and called it record-only. It is not. Everything running inside that graph reads
   `ctx.run_id` as its identity — `specialist_return`, `sales_recovery_executor`,
   `customer_send_choke`, `orchestrator_agent` — and **campaigns are created under it.** Repointing
   it silently changes `campaigns.run_id`, which is what the gate's own `assert_route` joins on and
   what the money path is keyed by. That is a migration-shaped decision, not an instrument. The
   warning now lives in the code next to the mismatch, with a test that keeps it there.
   **Open follow-up, needs its own row.**
4. `triage_decision` omits `task_kind` and `has_active_task`, both in memory at the emit site.
5. `_block_limit_exceeded` and `_block_prereq_or_policy_failed` share one `event_kind`.
6. **The gate tenants are gone** — `_REAP_AGE_HOURS = 1`. No retro-forensics; a re-drive is mandatory.

## Scope

1. **INSTRUMENT FIRST — Clau's explicit instruction: "I want the failure mechanism named before a
   patch."** Record what the route decision actually saw and chose, per turn, so a re-drive
   discriminates between hypotheses instead of re-observing the symptom. Must be shadow-safe: the
   instrument may not alter routing behaviour.
2. **Name the mechanism** with a file:line and a causal chain that survives adversarial refutation.
   Candidate classes under investigation: LLM sampling/model-selection variance on the routing call,
   a fail-soft `except` degrading a dispatch into a plain reply, a confidence threshold sitting near
   the decision boundary, context/history variance between runs, and pool/concurrency effects
   (VT-621 precedent).
3. **Fix the named mechanism.** Not a nudge — the repo's own META finding holds: deterministic
   fixes land, prompt nudges do not.
4. **Establish the real miss rate** on a stated denominator, before and after.

## Boundaries

- **A "fix" that reduces honest failures without increasing correct delegations is a regression,**
  not a fix. The two must be counted separately in the before/after or the number is meaningless.
- Correctness gates do not bend to make the miss rate look better: GST / ownership / consent /
  onboarded / opt-out stay exactly as they are. A route that delegates *more* by skipping a gate is
  the worst possible outcome of this row.
- No real customer send. Bogus fixtures only; never tenant `63211ce5` (Fazal's live number).
- Brain-touching → full-pack ×3.

## Exit gate

(a) The mechanism is **named** — file:line + causal chain — and the instrument shows it firing on a
re-drive, before any fix. (b) `sr_second_plan_queue_busy` returns `sales_recovery` **3/3** on
deployed dev. (c) Miss rate <5% on the SR pack, ×3, **with delegations and honest failures counted
separately** and the denominator stated. (d) Full-pack ×3 shows no new failure class, and in
particular no correctness gate weakened. (e) The retracted 24%/33%/40% figures are replaced by the
measured number everywhere they are quoted (`GO-LIVE-BLOCKERS.md` B1, promotion package).

## Why this is the top row

Clau's diagnosis, which I accept on the ranking even while correcting the number: *more knowledge is
not the constraint* — the O8 treatment null settled that. A paying owner who asks for a campaign and
is told "I couldn't do it" on a repeat of the same request does not have a product. This defect also

safety. Closing this row is what makes the next gate mean what it claims.
