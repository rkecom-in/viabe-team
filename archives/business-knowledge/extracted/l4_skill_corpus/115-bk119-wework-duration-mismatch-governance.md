## Situation

WeWork expanded through long leases while selling comparatively flexible memberships, accumulated losses and disclosed related-party issues; its 2019 IPO failed and it later entered bankruptcy.

## Decision pressure

Footprint and revenue growth are visible while long obligations, volatile demand and conflicts sit outside the headline metric.

## Mistake or risk

Coworking is not inherently unviable and bankruptcy had multiple causes. Transfer duration/liquidity mismatch, fixed commitments before proven unit economics, and conflict governance.

## Recommended next action

Match duration and repricing rights; stress occupancy and churn; map obligations by month; cap expansion by downside liquidity and mature-site economics; require independent related-party approval.

## Evidence needed

- Customer duration and churn
- Lease/debt obligations and breaks
- Mature-unit contribution and payback
- Downside liquidity stress
- Related-party register and approvals

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Cancellable revenue funds non-cancellable cost
- Growth requires continuous funding
- New units hide weak mature units
- Founder transactions lack independent review

## Agent lesson

Finance owns duration and liquidity; the Manager gates growth and enforces governance instead of treating fundraising as model proof.

## Hard-gate candidate

Long fixed obligations require duration analysis, downside liquidity, mature-unit economics and related-party clearance.
