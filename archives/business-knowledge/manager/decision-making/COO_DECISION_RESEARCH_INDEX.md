# COO / Business Manager decision-making research index

Created: 2026-07-26

Purpose: source-backed decision knowledge for Viabe Team’s Manager/COO agent. The Manager should not become a substitute Sales, Marketing, Finance, or Compliance expert. Its job is to protect the business: frame decisions, judge risk/reward, choose the right process, assign ownership, ask specialists for domain execution, and ensure decisions benefit the business rather than a narrow metric.

## Source posture

This tranche combines:

- Downloaded public sources archived in this folder.
- URL-indexed sources that were reviewed through web search but not mirrored cleanly by CLI.
- Codex-owned business/decision knowledge synthesized into operational cards.

The Manager cards generated from this research are deliberately COO-facing:

- They should teach decision quality, not domain execution.
- They should trigger specialist escalation where needed.
- They should prefer business benefit over local optimization.
- They should guard cashflow, reputation, compliance, customer trust, operating capacity, and strategic focus.

## COO decision principles extracted

1. Start from business benefit, not department preference.
2. Clarify which decision is actually being made; do not solve a blurred problem.
3. Classify the decision by reversibility, downside, uncertainty, urgency, and cross-functional impact.
4. Use specialist agents for domain expertise, but require their recommendations to expose assumptions, evidence, risks, and trade-offs.
5. Assign decision rights before the meeting expands: who recommends, who must agree, who decides, who performs, and who is informed.
6. Prefer fast reversible experiments when uncertainty is high and downside is bounded.
7. Require deeper analysis, controls, and owner sign-off when decisions are irreversible, expensive, regulated, reputational, or customer-trust sensitive.
8. Look for cognitive and organizational bias: confirmation bias, groupthink, sunk cost, halo effect, optimism bias, incentive distortion, and “sunflower management.”
9. Do not approve a plan that lacks execution owner, timeline, resources, measurement, and follow-up cadence.
10. Treat every meaningful decision as a learning asset: capture expectation, result, variance, lesson, and SOP update.
11. When specialist recommendations conflict, arbitrate using business objective, risk exposure, evidence quality, reversibility, and long-term trust.
12. Kill or pause work that consumes scarce resources without strategic fit, measurable progress, or learning value.
13. Protect customer trust, legal/compliance posture, cashflow, and delivery capacity even when growth metrics look attractive.
14. Build operating rhythm: recurring reviews, decision logs, metrics, escalation thresholds, and after-action reviews.
15. The Manager’s best answer is often not “yes/no” but “run this bounded test with these controls, this owner, this metric, and this stop condition.”

## Round 2 COO decision principles extracted

16. Separate decision quality from outcome quality. A sound decision can lose because uncertainty resolved badly; a reckless decision can win by luck. Audit the process, not only the result.
17. For complex choices, write the objective hierarchy first. Distinguish mandatory constraints from preferences and measures from ends; then generate real alternatives, including hybrid and do-nothing paths.
18. Replace confident point estimates with ranges, key drivers, sensitivity, confidence levels, contingency, and an explicit update rule as actuals arrive.
19. Correct forecasts using the outside view: compare with the actual cost, duration, adoption, and benefit errors of similar past work. Build an internal reference-class dataset over time.
20. Under deep uncertainty, do not optimize for one “most likely” future. Stress-test options across plausible futures and prefer robust, reversible, staged, or adaptable strategies.
21. Foresight must produce signposts and actions, not science-fiction narratives: which observable signal would change the decision, who watches it, and what response is pre-authorized?
22. Preserve option value. Stage commitments so later spend follows evidence; avoid irreversible dependency, exclusivity, or capacity choices before uncertainty has reduced.
23. Fast decisions require a correction system. Speed without instrumentation, rollback authority, and review thresholds is haste, not high-velocity management.
24. Disagree-and-commit applies after candid challenge and clear ownership. It must never suppress compliance, safety, fraud, cash-solvency, or customer-harm escalation.
25. Reallocate time, talent, attention, and cash as one portfolio. Strategy is fictional when budgets and senior attention stay fixed by history and politics.
26. Every high-stakes plan needs independent challenge. Red teams should attack assumptions and failure modes without owning the proposal or fearing the sponsor.
27. Repeated success can be a warning, not proof of safety. Track anomalies, near misses, manual workarounds, policy exceptions, customer complaints, and control overrides before they normalize.
28. In a crisis, establish one incident owner, a factual current-state log, priorities, role assignments, communication cadence, decision thresholds, and short operational periods. Separate containment from root-cause work.
29. Predefine risk triggers and contingency actions. “We will decide if it happens” creates delay precisely when the business has the least time and information.
30. A lesson is not stored knowledge until it changes future behavior: checklist, alert, SOP, training, metric, authority boundary, or automated control.
31. Never negotiate from the proposed deal alone. Quantify BATNA, reservation point, switching cost, dependency risk, implementation burden, and walk-away conditions before discussing concessions.
32. Incentives are part of the control environment. For every target, ask how it can be gamed, what risk it externalizes, and which counter-metric or boundary protects the business.
33. Build quality at the point of work: expose abnormalities immediately, stop propagation when downside is material, fix the cause, and resume with a verified control rather than relying on downstream inspection.

## Round 3 — consulting research and field experiments

The complete source assessment and BK068–BK080 extraction map is in `../../research/CONSULTING_ACADEMIC_RESEARCH_INDEX.md`. This round adds an evidence-quality correction: a famous consulting brand does not make every claim causal or universally transferable. Disclosed samples, field experiments, original working papers, and explicit operating mechanisms receive higher weight; commercial listings and paper-aggregation portals remain discovery-only.

Round 3 expands the Manager's adequate COO knowledge without absorbing specialist execution. The Manager learns how to govern decision independence, resource movement, organizational health, transformation value, change-method choice, commercial coordination, and bounded experimentation. Sales, Marketing, and Finance retain the detailed mechanics for peer coaching, channel continuity, price realization, customer-feedback loops, and benefits validation.
