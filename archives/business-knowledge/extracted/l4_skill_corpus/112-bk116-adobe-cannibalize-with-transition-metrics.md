## Situation

Adobe moved creative products from perpetual licences toward Creative Cloud subscriptions, accepting different revenue timing while tracking subscriptions and recurring revenue; by FY2015 it called perpetual creative revenue immaterial.

## Decision pressure

The legacy model reports more upfront revenue, so teams defend it even when durable customer and competitive economics favour recurring delivery.

## Mistake or risk

Not every product should be a subscription, and revenue across models is not comparable without timing adjustments. Adobe's product and brand conditions may not transfer.

## Recommended next action

Model old/new cohort cash flow, retention, acquisition and support; define conversion, recurring revenue, churn, usage and runway milestones; segment migration; protect continuity; explicitly authorise cannibalisation.

## Evidence needed

- Customer problem improved by model
- Old/new cohort cash contribution
- Conversion, retention, churn and usage
- Revenue and cash timing
- Migration support and downside runway

## Red flags / escalation triggers

- Subscription serves valuation optics only
- No continuing customer value
- Legacy falls before retention is visible
- Bookings rise while activation weakens

## Agent lesson

Finance owns transition economics, Sales and Marketing own migration, and the Manager judges durable customer and cash value rather than legacy optics.

## Hard-gate candidate

Business-model migration requires cohort economics, an accounting/cash bridge, customer continuity and stop/adjust triggers.
