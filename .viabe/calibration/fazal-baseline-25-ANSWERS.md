## A. PRICING & MARGIN

### Q01 — Kirana: the distributor's cash discount
**Answer: 2** (take the discount fully, funded by the overdraft)
> I would anyway be paying 4,00,000 on the 21st day to distributor or the bank, If I pay today, I pay
> 8,000 less and I have to give to the bank 3220 Interest for 21 days. So irrespective of the margins,
> if I am able to repay on time, and the stock gets cleared in the cycle, This can be a profitable
> approach.

*Arithmetic verified: 2% of ₹4L = ₹8,000; ₹4L × 14% × 21/365 = ₹3,222. Net +₹4,778. Conditions stated
by Fazal are explicit: repayment on time AND stock clearing within the cycle.*

### Q02 — Tiffin: input costs up 18%

> A base of 120 subscribers is too small and I won't be losing much to competitors, but since the
> price hike, its a good chance to position myself distinct from the competitors running on low
> margin, and I would prefer raising the price to 3400 and add a visible improvement. Present myself

### Q03 — Boutique: the marketplace bargain

> The marketplace is a costly but contributes to 40% of sales, so we can't exit or ignore it. It is
> better to keep the volume while systematically shifting repeat purchases to own site, by offering

*Adds a mechanism not in the option text: a discount card in the parcel as the shift instrument.*

### Q04 — Kirana: own-brand cannibalisation

## E. OPERATIONS & SUPPLY

### Q17 — Peak-season stock-out
**Answer: 3** (small quantity from the alternate, for regulars)
> The item drives 18% of revenue and it is peak season, so a full 10-day stock-out is costly in lost
> sales and in training customers to look elsewhere. At the same time, buying large volume at a 19%
> higher cost that completely erases the line's margin turns every unit into a pure loss and is hard
> to justify. A limited purchase from the alternate supplier solves the immediate problem with
> controlled risk. Better to protect shelf presence in a limited, targeted way.

### Q18 — Delivery: own boy or aggregator
**Answer: 3** (own boy for regulars, aggregator for overflow/distance)
> At 25 deliveries a day you are already in the range where a dedicated boy can be cost-competitive,
> yet you still have spare capacity and growth ahead. A pure own-boy model locks in fixed cost and
> management overhead; a pure aggregator model permanently gives away 18% on every order and weakens
> direct customer contact.

### Q19 — The exclusivity offer
**Answer: 5** (refuse exclusivity, take a volume-linked rebate)
> An 8% price improvement is attractive, but locking 30% of our revenue into a single supplier for
> 12 months creates dangerous concentration risk. A volume‑linked rebate gives us most of the
> economic benefit without giving up the freedom to switch if supply, price, or demand changes

### Q20 — The quality cluster
**Answer: 4** (pull it; demand replacement or credit from the supplier first)
> Six complaints in a week on the same batch is a clear quality signal; continuing to sell exposes us
> to product‑liability risk and damages trust. We have to stop selling immediately, document the
> problem, and push the supplier hard for replacement or credit based on that evidence.

# EXTRACTED DECISION RULES — the Tier-1 seed

Derived by Clau from all 25 rationales, 2026-08-11. **These, not the option numbers, are the asset.**
Every rule cites the questions that produced it so it can be re-derived or disputed.

**R1 — Cash is committed only against a bounded, evidenced return. Not "preserve cash."**
He is not debt-averse: he borrows at 14% (Q01) and at 22% (Q07) when the payback lands inside the
cycle and he has computed it. He refuses to tie up ₹35,000 without a trial (Q04), refuses to touch
the float for an unrelated need (Q08), and takes ₹6L of stock only once the inventory risk is
transferred (Q25). The constraint is **unfunded, unbounded, or unmeasured** exposure — not spending.
*(Q01, Q04, Q07, Q08, Q25)*

**R2 — Buy information before buying commitment. The strongest and most repeated pattern.**
Six of 25 convert an irreversible decision into a reversible one by inserting a measurement step:
trial before stocking (Q04), tracked link or decline (Q14), verify before dispatch (Q15), evidence
before the supplier claim (Q20), cameras before accusation (Q21), a proven manager before the second
outlet (Q23). *(Q04, Q14, Q15, Q20, Q21, Q23)*

**R3 — Refuse concentration and lock-in; pay a premium for optionality.**
No 12-month exclusivity even at 8% (Q19). Stop growing a receivable at 30% of revenue (Q11). Keep
marketplace volume but migrate the customer to the owned channel (Q03). Hybrid delivery rather than
dependence on either model (Q18). *(Q03, Q11, Q18, Q19)*

**R4 — Relationships are protected, but concessions are always conditional or staged.**
Pay the defaulting distributor in full — goodwill extended ONCE, contingent on the fix (Q05). A menu
so the counterparty self-selects by capacity (Q06). A path to clear the backlog, not forgiveness
(Q11). Satisfy the customer first, but keep the advance unless it is a one-off regular (Q09). Half
the raise now, half against targets (Q22). **No unconditional give appears anywhere in 25 answers.**
*(Q05, Q06, Q09, Q11, Q22)*

**R5 — Compete on position, never on broad price.**
Raise price and add visible value to become the premium option (Q02). Match only the small set of
compared items (Q16). Protect shelf presence in a targeted way rather than sell at zero margin (Q17).
*(Q02, Q16, Q17)*

**R6 — Trust and purpose-limitation are not tradeable.**
Refuse the ₹50,000 data sale on purpose-limitation grounds, reasoning to consent unprompted — the
scenario never mentioned DPDP (Q12). Report fake reviews and mobilise genuine ones rather than
respond in kind (Q10). No accusation without proof (Q21). Record everything even where informality
is cheaper (Q24). **In each, he chose the option that costs money or effort to hold the principle.**
*(Q10, Q12, Q21, Q24)*

## SCORING NOTE — added during capture, before any comparison

Two answer SHAPES appeared that the instrument's scoring design did not anticipate, and both must be
handled explicitly or the comparison will understate agreement:

1. **MENU (Q06, "4 and 6")** — two options offered simultaneously, the counterparty self-selects.
   Distinct from a hybrid, which blends into one action.
2. **CONDITIONAL SEQUENCE (Q07, "4, then 1")** — a preference order with a trigger. A comparator that
   picks only option 4 has the same FIRST rule but no fallback; that scores
   *compatible-rule-partial*, not *same rule* and not *different rule*.
3. **SEGMENTED CONDITIONAL (Q09, "4 then 6, and 1 in specific cases")** — a sequence PLUS a branch on
   counterparty type (tough customer vs one-off regular). The richest shape seen, and the one a
   single-option comparator can least represent. Score the branch trigger separately from the
   default path.

Both must be offered to the comparator (Codex) in its prompt, or the format asymmetry — not the
judgment — will produce artificial disagreement. **Codex's prompt must state that menus, hybrids and
conditional sequences are permitted answers.** This is a real instrument defect caught mid-collection;
recorded rather than silently patched.
