# VT-759 — the beats enforce mode never had

## What this row said first, and why it was wrong

Rostered 2026-08-14 as a **scenario-quality** defect: three gate (d) failures where an English literal
(`hold off`, `one at a time`, `sure`) was asserted against a Hinglish reply, "the BEHAVIOUR each
assert stands for was satisfied and the assert failed on a word."

Re-driven on deployed dev 2026-08-15 before touching anything. Two of the three are not that:

```
readiness_ask_then_defer_and_resume  step 0   route=none   surface=manager
profile_preview_then_confirm         step 0   route=none   surface=manager
```

`route: none` + `surface: manager` means **no journey beat ran at all**. Both asserts name text that
only exists inside deterministic templates — `journey._DEFER_MSG` and `journey._READINESS_ASK`, both
reached only through `_maybe_handle_post_profile_flow`. Dev runs `TEAM_MANAGER_LOOP_MODE=enforce`,
whose gate is `enforce_journey_gate.maybe_handle_enforce_journey_turn`, and that gate says so in its
own words: *"Deliberately NARROW: it does NOT delegate the full `_maybe_handle_post_profile_flow`
machine."* DF4 ported exactly ONE beat — the AFFIRM at `ready_asked` (a clear connect signal → an
honest offer). The DECLINE half and the profile-card ACKNOWLEDGEMENT were never ported.

**So the asserts were right and the product was missing the beat.** Rewriting them to match what the
brain improvised would have been precisely the "loosen an assert to make a run pass" this row's own
Boundaries section forbade — and would have filed a real product gap under a vocabulary story.

This is the second time in two days the count-first reading lost to the mechanism-first one
(`CL-2026-08-15-roster-on-mechanism-count-needs-x3`). The tell was available before the re-drive and
I did not use it: the scenario notes said "verbatim template", and a verbatim template cannot fail on
vocabulary.

## What the owner actually got

| turn | what fired | what the owner read |
|---|---|---|
| decline at `ready_asked` | nothing — brain | *"Theek hai, abhi pause kar dete hain. Jab aap ready hon, yahin se continue kar lenge."* |
| ack at `profile_previewed` | nothing — brain | *"Is approval ka exact plan ya option mujhe yahan dikh nahi raha; uska naam ya ek line dobara bhej dijiye…"* |

The first is *semantically* close — but it is composed rather than the honest template (which states
what is still missing from the registry's `plan_blocked_reason`, the single source of truth,
CL-2026-07-03-plan-governance), and critically **it never set `__flow__ = deferred`**. The decline was
answered and then forgotten: the flow was not resumable, so the resume half of the scenario was
passing by luck of the brain, not by the machine.

The second is worse than a phrasing miss. The owner acknowledged their profile card and the Manager
answered as though an **approval** were pending, asking them to re-send an option they never sent.
Fabricated context plus a re-ask, on the turn immediately after the owner said yes — the same
honesty-floor family as VT-756 and VT-761.

## What was built

**Two beats in `enforce_journey_gate.py`, each as narrow as DF4:**

1. `_maybe_post_profile_defer` — flow ∈ {`ready_asked`, `intro_sent`, `trial_sent`} and a
   DETERMINISTIC decline floor hit (`_is_decline and not _is_affirm`) → `journey._flow_defer`, which
   sends the honest template AND arms the resume. Deliberately NOT `_resolve_readiness_intent`: its
   ambiguous→affirm mapping is the wrong default here and its classifier call would put an LLM inside
   a beat that exists to be deterministic. Opt-out/DSR is checked first — a STOP is never a "later".
2. `_maybe_post_profile_ack_advance` — flow == `profile_previewed` and a non-interrogative AFFIRM
   floor hit → `journey._flow_ask_readiness`, advancing the sentinel so DF4's connect beat owns the
   next turn.

**What was deliberately NOT ported:** the legacy `intro → trial-terms → offer` chain. That is the
machine DF4 declined (its single-pick Shopify pitch is the measured fabrication). Skipping
`_flow_agent_trial` loses nothing on the compliance floor — **VT-722 already states the trial terms
and records the `trial_terms` assertion at the `data_inputs_enable` chooser, deterministically and
mode-independently**, which is why that beat's absence in enforce mode has not cost an owner the
terms. Checked before deciding, not assumed.

**Third scenario, the one real vocabulary case.** `sr_stop_then_resume` asserted `'sure'` to pin a
re-confirm question. Replaced with a new `assert_is_question` primitive — a SPEECH ACT asserted
structurally (`?` is the same character in English, Hinglish and Devanagari) rather than by a synonym
list, which the standing no-lists rule forbids. Narrower than "asked the right question" on purpose:
the money-safety half is carried by observables that cannot drift with vocabulary — the
false-send-claim needles and `expect_sent_count: 0`.

## The pack-wide sweep (exit gate c)

33 English-literal `assert_contains` needles live in scenarios that drive a non-English owner turn.
Rather than judge them by inspection, they were scored against the evidence already on disk — gate
(d) ×3, 870 step-runs. **15 assert_contains sites failed at least once:**

| needle | sites | ×3 rate | what it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `draft` / `drafted` | 6 SR scenarios | 1–3/3 | **NOT vocabulary.** Every one co-occurs with `assert_not_d1` firing on *"Got it — I'm on it and I'll update you shortly"* plus `route=none` and no campaign row. This is the async-stall / answer-in-turn class (VT-752). The assert is correct and is doing its job. |
| `8` | m_conversation_multi_request_mixed_ask | 3/3 | The honest-zero class (VT-756): *"I don't yet have a customer count"* on a tenant with 8 seeded customers. Assert correct. |
| `http`, `don't see the connection` | i_sheets_mapping_confirm_happy_path | 3/3 | VT-761, already rostered. Assert correct. |
| `approval` | 2 status-check scenarios | 1/3 | Variance, not a class. Watch, do not act (the ×3 rule). |
| `hold off`, `one at a time` | 2 onboarding scenarios | 3/3 | **This row.** Product gap, now fixed. |
| `sure` | sr_stop_then_resume | 2/3 | The one true vocabulary defect → `assert_is_question`. |

The remaining 18 needles never failed in 870 step-runs. Their residual risk is named rather than
waved away: **inflected English verbs** (`stopped`, `drafted`) and **English weekday names**
(`tuesday`, in `longhaul_retention_multifact`) are the shapes that could still fail on a Hinglish
drift; digits and proper nouns (`8`, `karol bagh`, `myshopify.com`) are language-invariant and safe by
construction. Nothing was pre-emptively rewritten — a needle with three clean passes and no mechanism
to point at is exactly what the standing rule says to leave alone.

**So exit gate (d) reverses the row's original claim.** VT-759 said gate (d)'s 86.9% was a FLOOR that
rises to 88.5% once three bad asserts are corrected. That is wrong. Of the three, **one** was an
instrument error (~0.8pt); the other two were real product defects. 86.9% was an honest number. It
should rise now because two defects were fixed, not because the instrument was recalibrated.

## Exit gate

(a) ✅ Both beats fire on the exact gate (d) messages, and each arms the correct next state — 12 unit
tests, including a wiring test (a unit-green beat the gate never calls is the VT-720 dead-fix class).
(b) ✅ Narrowness proven: affirm, ambiguous, opt-out-shaped, wrong-flow and no-recipient inputs all
return None → the brain. (c) ✅ Sweep published above. (d) ⏳ Both scenarios pass on deployed dev on
their ORIGINAL asserts — pending the batch push. **The proof standard is the original assert text: if
`hold off` and `one at a time` do not appear, the beat did not fire and this row is not done.**
